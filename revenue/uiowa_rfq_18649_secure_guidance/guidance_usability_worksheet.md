# UIOWA-058 — Secure-development guidance usability worksheet

Use this worksheet to assess the **guidance and support system**, not to grade individuals.

| Dimension | Evidence to request | Useful question | Interpretation boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discoverability | Handbook/search location, starter template, onboarding links | Where would a practitioner look during ordinary maintenance? | A document that exists but cannot be found may not be usable in practice |
| Applicability cues | Decision triggers, scope, examples | How does someone know this guidance applies to the change in front of them? | Generic principles are not automatically actionable |
| Reusable pattern | Pattern/checklist/template and worked example | Is there a repeatable way to apply the guidance? | Do not require one technology or framework |
| Ownership/current status | Named owner, version/review history, superseded links | Who keeps this current and how can a practitioner recognize the current version? | Written status is evidence about the document, not proof of team practice |
| Specialist access | Route, expected trigger, response mechanism | When ambiguity exceeds routine guidance, how does the team get help? | Missing specialist evidence is a support-system gap, not an employee deficiency |
| Onboarding/reinforcement | New-hire path, examples, office hours, communities of practice | How is guidance encountered after onboarding and during maintenance? | Attendance does not prove application |
| Practice evidence | Representative design/review/test artifacts | Can a real recent change be traced to the guidance or equivalent reasoning? | Sample findings must stay bounded to the sampled work |

## Interview use

For each fictional or real representative change:
1. Ask where the practitioner would seek guidance before discussing content.
2. Ask what decision facts matter and why.
3. Ask for a reusable pattern/template if one exists.
4. Ask what ambiguity would trigger specialist help.
5. Ask what artifact from completed work would establish actual application.
6. Record missing/unknown evidence explicitly; do not convert silence into a practice failure.

## Recommendation design

Recommendations should improve the system around practitioners: consolidation, search/discoverability, ownership, clearer triggers, concise patterns, onboarding integration, specialist routing, or feedback loops. Avoid employee rankings and avoid prescribing a commercial product.
