# Cross-cutting assessment themes — UIOWA-083

This is an editable production pack for writing a department-level assessment from
individual findings. It supplies a method, a reusable theme card, and three complete
worked narratives. **All worked evidence is fictional. Nothing in this pack describes
an observed University of Iowa practice or establishes a maturity rating.**

## Start here

1. Read the synthesis method below and copy one card from
   [theme_template.md](theme_template.md).
2. Open [worked_narratives.md](worked_narratives.md). Follow a narrative's finding
   links to its evidence passages. The three cases cover a shared strength, uneven
   practice, and equivalent outcomes achieved through different implementations.
3. Replace fictional records with approved assessment findings and their existing
   evidence IDs, versions, locators and observation periods. Keep the original
   finding text available; the theme is an additional interpretation.
4. Separate the proposed department response from group-specific follow-ups. Record
   unresolved disagreement and what observation would change the interpretation.
5. Give the theme card and its traceability table to the report writer and review
   coordinator together. Keep source evidence in its approved working location.

## Method: from findings to a decision

### Frame a question with a defined scope

Start with an outcome such as whether another practitioner can identify and operate
the released change. State which services, types of change and observation window
the findings cover. A department theme may describe a shared dependency or a useful
common decision without claiming that every team behaves alike.

### Preserve distinct units of evidence

Classify each finding as a shared capability, repeated local practice, inherited
dependency, local exception or unresolved observation. One shared record used by
three groups remains one record. A copy, a summary and an interview citing that
record are not three independent confirmations. Keep evidence collected at different
times separate until the author can explain why it describes the same practice.

### Compare the outcome before comparing the mechanism

Write down what successful performance would look like for the actual service.
Then examine what each group's evidence establishes. Manual steps, automation and
shared services can all support the same outcome. A tool choice is neither a
department strength nor a gap by itself. Different criticality, release cadence or
data boundaries may justify different operating choices.

### Choose the narrowest supportable proposition

| Evidence pattern | Useful department proposition | Overstatement to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| One shared capability, plus evidence of use in multiple groups | The shared capability supports the sampled groups; adoption and performance have separate evidence | Three independently mature groups |
| Same observable gap in multiple local examples | A common improvement is plausible within the observed sample; local causes remain distinct | Every group has the same root cause |
| Different mechanisms, comparable observed outcome | Common acceptance evidence can coexist with local implementation choices | All groups need the same platform |
| A policy and a missing operating record | The sampled operating evidence is incomplete; ask for the missing record | The practice does not happen |
| Conflicting accounts or periods | The conclusion is unresolved or time-specific, with a named follow-up | The more polished account is correct |

Do not average ordinal maturity labels to manufacture a department score. Coverage,
confidence and maturity remain separate inputs. If an upstream assessment supplies a
characterization, cite its method and scope; this writing pack does not calculate one.

### Explain consequences without inventing outcomes

Separate observed effects from plausible consequences. For example, a missing
support-owner record establishes a traceability gap in that record. It may create
handoff delay, but it does not establish that a delay occurred. State a measured
effect only when the cited evidence includes the period, unit and relevant baseline.

### Propose a decision that fits the finding

Identify the accountable role, the initial bounded practice change, prerequisites,
effort assumptions and an observable completion measure. A department response can
be a shared minimum evidence standard with local implementation choices. Assign a
0–90, 90–180 or 180+ day planning horizon only as a proposal; it is not an appointment
or a confirmed staffing commitment. Preserve any justified decision to defer.

### Keep the revision trail usable

Use stable theme, finding, evidence and recommendation IDs from the working register.
A wording edit does not change source evidence. A new source or changed finding
requires reconsidering the linked proposition and recommendation. Record the previous
version, what changed, why, and any unresolved review comment. Do not silently close
a disagreement because the narrative has been shortened.

## Completion review

A reviewer should be able to answer four questions from the theme and linked table:
what is common; what differs; why the distinction matters; and what decision follows.
Every material factual sentence must lead to a finding and an exact source passage.
Every recommendation must name its assumption and intended outcome. Preserve any
unknown, counterexample or unresolved interpretation that would change the decision.

For publication, remove internal drafting instructions from the reader-facing copy,
retain material limitations, and deliver it through the engagement's approved delivery
surface. Repository paths in this pack are internal authoring references.
