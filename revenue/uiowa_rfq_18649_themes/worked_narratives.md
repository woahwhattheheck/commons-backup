# Worked department themes — wholly synthetic writing examples

**SYNTHETIC EXERCISE ONLY.** Every event, source, count, passage, interview, finding and recommendation below was invented to demonstrate a writing method. ESS, RIS and IAM are scenario labels borrowed from the assessment frame; nothing here describes the University of Iowa, its staff, systems, suppliers or actual performance. Do not present these examples as assessment results.

The embedded evidence cards are the complete sources for these examples. Their locators identify passages within fictional records, not external documents that the reader is expected to retrieve. Each quoted passage is reproduced exactly as defined for this exercise. The three themes are separate scenarios; their samples must never be pooled.

Evidence IDs (SYN-E…), finding IDs (SYN-F…) and recommendation IDs (SYN-R…) are stable within this document. Links connect recommendations to findings and findings to source passages. Source identity, assessment reach and independent corroboration are separate concepts: one source can cover several groups without becoming several independent sources.

Day ranges below are illustrative implementation windows measured from a future agreed start, **not appointments, commitments or an approved project schedule**. Effort ranges are unsupported planning assumptions to be validated by an assigned owner. Recommendations convey no submission, release, procurement, signature, payment or revenue authority.

## Theme 1 — Preserve a shared operational strength while repairing a local exception

**Scenario question:** Does one shared incident coordination service provide useful coverage across the three groups, and where does the evidence qualify that conclusion?

**Fictional assessment boundary:** Exercise days E01–E28; the shared service's 18 recorded incidents only. The sample excludes incidents handled outside that service and does not establish annual availability, service reliability or end-user satisfaction.

### SYN-E1-01

**Source:** Fictional shared incident register, edition 1. **Locator:** summary rows 1–3 and incident IAM-04, update field. **Reach:** ESS, RIS and IAM. **Independence:** one operational register; its group slices are not separate corroborating sources.

> E01–E28: 18 incidents entered the shared coordination service: ESS 8, RIS 6, IAM 4. All 18 received a named human owner within the internal 15-minute assignment target. Seventeen received their next update by the recorded due time. IAM-04 received that update 22 minutes late. Assignment time starts at service entry, not at the original onset of the incident.

### SYN-E1-02

**Source:** Fictional shared-service operating note, edition 2. **Locator:** section 2, paragraphs 1–2. **Reach:** all three groups. **Independence:** a design description; it is not additional evidence that the service met its targets.

> One coordinator rota serves ESS, RIS and IAM. The same incident form records a named owner, next-update time and application contact. Group labels route notifications; they do not create separate coordination teams or separate service-level measurements.

### SYN-E1-03

**Source:** Fictional joint retrospective record, edition 1. **Locator:** agenda item 3, accepted observation and dissent. **Reach:** local participants' accounts. **Independence:** interview evidence about usefulness and a failure explanation; the incident count still comes solely from the register.

> ESS and RIS participants described the named-owner field as useful for handoffs. The IAM participant did not agree that notification coverage was dependable: the IAM-04 message reached the coordinator but not the application contact. Participants proposed exercising that route and its backup; neither route had yet been exercised in this review.

### SYN-F1-01

**Finding:** A single shared coordination practice demonstrated consistent initial ownership in the recorded sample, with a bounded update exception. [SYN-E1-01](#syn-e1-01) supports 18/18 timely assignments and 17/18 timely next updates; [SYN-E1-02](#syn-e1-02) explains the common operating mechanism.

**Counting rule:** Record one shared-service observation reaching three groups. Do not report three independent strengths, three service samples of 18, or 54 successful assignments. The ESS/RIS/IAM counts of 8/6/4 partition the same 18 incidents. The operating note and retrospective add context, not additional successful cases.

### SYN-F1-02

**Finding:** The IAM application-contact route needs specific follow-up. [SYN-E1-01](#syn-e1-01) identifies the late update, while [SYN-E1-03](#syn-e1-03) records the participant's explanation and disagreement. The explanation remains an interview account until the notification path is examined or exercised.

**Limits:** A timely assignment does not prove incident resolution. A late next update does not establish that every IAM notification fails. Routing configuration, contact availability and incomplete update records are alternative explanations to examine, not established causes. The two positive participant accounts do not negate the IAM account or establish a satisfaction rate.

### Report-ready narrative — synthetic example

In this fictional sample, the shared incident coordination service provided a consistent point of initial ownership across ESS, RIS and IAM: all 18 recorded incidents received a named owner within the service's 15-minute target. Seventeen received their next update on time. This is one shared operational strength with reach across three groups, rather than three independent observations of performance. The IAM-04 update exception and the IAM participant's account identify a specific application-contact route for follow-up. Preserve the shared ownership practice while checking that route and its backup; the available evidence does not support redesigning the service or claiming comprehensive notification reliability. ([SYN-F1-01](#syn-f1-01), [SYN-F1-02](#syn-f1-02))

### SYN-R1-01

**Recommendation:** Retain the common rota and incident fields; examine the IAM application-contact route and complete a small notification exercise. **Finding links:** [SYN-F1-01](#syn-f1-01), [SYN-F1-02](#syn-f1-02).

| Planning element | Illustrative implementation |
|---|---|
| Accountable role | Shared-service owner; IAM application contact supplies local route knowledge. |
| Days 0–30 | Reconcile IAM-04 against the notification configuration; document the actual route, backup and expected receipt evidence. Preserve the participant's account if the cause remains unresolved. |
| Days 31–60 | Conduct three agreed exercises covering primary route, backup route and an unavailable primary contact; retain timestamps and intended-recipient acknowledgements. |
| Days 61–90 | Examine all eligible incidents in one subsequent 28-day window; if none occur, report no live-case evidence and keep exercises separate. |
| Effort assumption | 2–4 combined person-days, assuming the existing service owner can access its own configuration and the change fits its normal maintenance process. Validate before planning. |
| Measures | Report acknowledged notifications / attempted exercise notifications, and timely next updates / eligible live incidents, with denominators and exceptions. Do not combine exercises and live cases. |
| Proposed completion test | All three exercises produce the expected receipt evidence, or each unsuccessful case has a named follow-up. Completion of the exercise is distinct from resolution of every defect. |

**Preserved dissent and stop condition:** Keep the IAM concern visible until route evidence addresses it. If IAM-04's explanation cannot be confirmed, state “cause unresolved”; do not replace it with an assumed configuration fault. A zero-incident follow-up window cannot establish improved live reliability.

## Theme 2 — Address inconsistent recorded practice without scoring missing evidence as failure

**Scenario question:** Where do sampled changes show a genuine departure from a shared review expectation, and where is the record merely incomplete?

**Fictional assessment boundary:** One local sample of 17 changes: 15 normal changes and two urgent restorations. Selection was convenient, not random; the proportions describe this sample only. This scenario has no relationship to the incidents in Theme 1.

### SYN-E2-01

**Source:** Fictional change-review working agreement, edition 3. **Locator:** rules N1 and U1. **Reach:** ESS, RIS and IAM. **Independence:** the criterion against which records are assessed, not proof of compliance.

> N1: A normal change has a peer review recorded before deployment. U1: An urgent restoration may proceed under the named restoration owner's exception; its retrospective review is recorded within two business days. A missing review record establishes a documentation question, not proof that no review occurred.

### SYN-E2-02

**Source:** Fictional ESS change extract, edition 1. **Locator:** sample rows ESS-C01 through ESS-C06, review/deployment fields. **Reach:** six normal ESS changes. **Independence:** one local change-register extract.

> Six sampled normal changes each contain a retrievable peer-review record timestamped before deployment. No urgent restoration was included in this ESS sample. The extract does not describe changes outside these six rows.

### SYN-E2-03

**Source:** Fictional RIS change extract, edition 1. **Locator:** rows RIS-C01 through RIS-C04 and RIS-U01 through RIS-U02. **Reach:** four normal RIS changes and two urgent RIS restorations. **Independence:** one local extract; its urgent rows are not counted again as normal changes.

> Three of four sampled normal changes have a retrievable pre-deployment review record. RIS-C04's review record was not available to the assessor. Both urgent restorations identify the permitted exception and restoration owner. RIS-U01's retrospective is recorded after one business day; RIS-U02's is recorded after three business days. The exception permits two business days.

### SYN-E2-04

**Source:** Fictional IAM change extract, edition 1. **Locator:** rows IAM-C01 through IAM-C05, approval-event fields. **Reach:** five normal IAM changes. **Independence:** one local extract; it records outcomes rather than proving the design of every deployment control.

> All five sampled normal changes have a retrievable peer-review approval event before deployment. The approval-event record identifies the peer reviewer and change ID. No urgent restoration was sampled; no conclusion about IAM urgent-restoration handling is recorded.

### SYN-E2-05

**Source:** Fictional RIS review meeting note, edition 1. **Locator:** item 4, owner response and assessor disposition. **Reach:** RIS-C04 and RIS-U02. **Independence:** a participant account, not a replacement for either missing or recorded timestamps.

> The RIS owner recalled a verbal peer review for RIS-C04, but no supporting record was supplied. For RIS-U02, the owner accepted the three-business-day timestamp and preferred preserving restoration speed over adding a pre-restoration approval. The assessor agreed that the recorded issue concerns retrospective timing, not the permitted decision to restore service first.

### SYN-F2-01

**Finding:** Evidence of normal-change review is mostly retrievable in this sample, with one unresolved documentation gap. [SYN-E2-02](#syn-e2-02), [SYN-E2-03](#syn-e2-03) and [SYN-E2-04](#syn-e2-04) support 14 documented pre-deployment reviews among 15 normal changes: ESS 6/6, RIS 3/4 and IAM 5/5. The remaining RIS case is **unknown**, not a demonstrated absence of review or a low maturity score.

**Dissent handling:** Record the RIS owner's recollection from [SYN-E2-05](#syn-e2-05) alongside the unavailable record. Do not silently choose the recollection as proof or describe it as disproved. This finding concerns retrievability and cannot rank the groups' overall engineering quality.

### SYN-F2-02

**Finding:** One of the two sampled RIS urgent restorations exceeded the agreed retrospective window. The three-business-day RIS-U02 record in [SYN-E2-03](#syn-e2-03) differs from the two-business-day expectation in [SYN-E2-01](#syn-e2-01); RIS-U01 met that expectation. This is a confirmed local timing departure, distinct from the unknown normal-change record in [SYN-F2-01](#syn-f2-01).

**Local exception retained:** Urgent restoration before review is explicitly permitted. The owner raises a concern about adding pre-restoration approval; the assessor distinguishes that concern from retrospective timing. Workload, unclear ownership, missed reminders or another cause might explain RIS-U02, but none is established by the source. No conclusion about ESS or IAM urgent practice is available because neither sample contains an urgent case.

### Report-ready narrative — synthetic example

The fictional change sample shows a broadly retrievable review trail with two different follow-up needs. Fourteen of fifteen normal changes have a recorded review before deployment. The remaining RIS record is unavailable, and the owner's recollection of a verbal review remains uncorroborated; this is an evidence gap rather than proof of absent review. Separately, one of two RIS urgent restorations recorded its retrospective after three business days against a two-business-day expectation. The response should preserve the permitted restoration-first exception while improving review-record retrieval and ownership of retrospective deadlines. The sample does not justify a maturity ranking or a department-wide approval redesign. ([SYN-F2-01](#syn-f2-01), [SYN-F2-02](#syn-f2-02))

### SYN-R2-01

**Recommendation:** Resolve or explicitly retain RIS-C04's evidence gap and make retrospective ownership visible for future RIS urgent restorations. **Finding links:** [SYN-F2-01](#syn-f2-01), [SYN-F2-02](#syn-f2-02).

| Planning element | Illustrative implementation |
|---|---|
| Accountable role | RIS change-process owner, with the restoration lead and a peer reviewer; ESS/IAM owners advise only where their record-linking practice is useful. |
| Days 0–30 | Request the existing RIS-C04 record and record “unavailable” if it cannot be recovered. For each new urgent restoration, identify the retrospective owner and due time in the current workflow. |
| Days 31–60 | Review up to the next ten normal RIS changes for retrievable evidence and every urgent restoration in the window for retrospective timing. Report a smaller sample if fewer occur; do not create changes or urgent cases to fill it. |
| Days 61–90 | Examine exceptions with the RIS owner; retain the restoration-first provision. Adjust workload or reminders only where the observed cause supports that response. |
| Effort assumption | 2–5 combined person-days, assuming a link/owner/due-date adjustment is possible in the existing record system and no new approval service is introduced. |
| Measures | Normal changes with retrievable pre-change evidence / normal changes sampled; urgent retrospectives within two business days / urgent restorations sampled; separately list unavailable records and confirmed late reviews. |
| Proposed completion test | RIS-C04 has a documented evidence disposition, all sampled urgent cases identify an owner and due time, and the follow-up readout keeps unknown evidence separate from known timing departures. |

**Limits on interpretation:** A better retrieval rate is not proof of better review quality. Zero urgent cases means urgent timing was not observed. If the owner proposes changing the two-day expectation, treat that as a separate decision with reasons; never rewrite the original criterion to make the historical exception disappear.

## Theme 3 — Recognize equivalent observed outcomes across different technical stacks

**Scenario question:** Do different delivery mechanisms provide the same selected operating outcomes, and does this evidence justify standardizing the underlying technology?

**Fictional assessment boundary:** Fifteen prepared demonstration cases, five per group, evaluated against the same three outcome checks. These are demonstrations, not production incidents, randomly selected releases or load tests. This scenario is separate from Themes 1 and 2.

### SYN-E3-01

**Source:** Fictional demonstration protocol, edition 1. **Locator:** checks O1–O3 and exclusions. **Reach:** all fifteen cases. **Independence:** the shared assessment rubric, not an outcome observation.

> For each prepared case, O1 asks whether the change can be linked to a reviewable request; O2 asks whether the validation result can be retrieved; O3 asks whether a named operational contact acknowledges the handoff within 30 minutes of demonstration start. All groups use these same checks. Production reliability, rollback under failure, security effectiveness, cost and throughput are outside this exercise.

### SYN-E3-02

**Source:** Fictional assessor demonstration worksheet, edition 1. **Locator:** case ranges ESS-D01–D05, RIS-D01–D05 and IAM-D01–D05; outcome summary. **Reach:** five prepared cases per group. **Independence:** one assessor worksheet with fifteen observations; three group views do not constitute three independent replications.

> ESS: five of five cases satisfy O1, O2 and O3 using a managed release pipeline. RIS: five of five satisfy O1, O2 and O3 using a batch scheduler and an operator checklist. IAM: five of five satisfy O1, O2 and O3 using an API-driven deployment runner. The assessor retrieved each request and validation result and recorded each contact acknowledgement within 30 minutes. No failed-release or load scenario was exercised.

### SYN-E3-03

**Source:** Fictional architecture discussion note, edition 1. **Locator:** item 2, mechanism descriptions and effort caveat. **Reach:** representative accounts from the three groups. **Independence:** explanatory testimony; effort comments were not measured in the demonstrations.

> ESS and IAM representatives described automated preparation of their handoff records. The RIS representative described preparing the checklist manually and estimated 20–40 minutes of preparation per case. Comparable preparation-time records were not supplied for any group. The RIS representative favored keeping the scheduler because of its current workload fit; the group made no technology-selection decision.

### SYN-F3-01

**Finding:** Different mechanisms achieved the same three selected outcomes in the prepared cases. [SYN-E3-01](#syn-e3-01) establishes comparable checks; [SYN-E3-02](#syn-e3-02) records five successful cases per group. The supported conclusion is functional equivalence **for those checks and cases**, not equivalence of all platform capabilities or production performance.

**Counting rule:** Report fifteen observed cases in one demonstration exercise. Each case satisfied three checks; forty-five passed checks are not forty-five independent deployments. Do not turn the all-pass prepared sample into a production success rate.

### SYN-F3-02

**Finding:** The exercise does not establish a cost, throughput or reliability advantage for any stack. [SYN-E3-03](#syn-e3-03) suggests a question about manual preparation but provides no comparable measured effort. That estimate is not a demonstrated efficiency deficit and cannot support a savings calculation, supplier preference or platform migration recommendation.

**Dissent retained:** The RIS representative's workload-fit preference is a relevant local consideration, not proof that the scheduler is universally preferable. Similarly, automation in ESS and IAM is a mechanism description, not proof that those approaches are cheaper or better suited to RIS.

### Report-ready narrative — synthetic example

The fictional demonstrations show that different technical stacks can deliver comparable operating outcomes. Across five prepared cases per group, ESS's release pipeline, RIS's scheduler and checklist, and IAM's deployment runner each provided a retrievable request, validation result and timely operational acknowledgement. That common outcome evidence supports a shared assessment vocabulary while leaving implementation choices open. Preparation effort, failure recovery and production behavior were not compared, so the exercise provides no basis for selecting a preferred stack or mandating standardization. A useful next step is to make the same outcome evidence easier to compare in subsequent work, then investigate any specific measured gap. ([SYN-F3-01](#syn-f3-01), [SYN-F3-02](#syn-f3-02))

### SYN-R3-01

**Recommendation:** Agree a common description of outcome evidence while allowing each group to keep its existing delivery mechanism. Collect comparable effort observations before considering any efficiency intervention. **Finding links:** [SYN-F3-01](#syn-f3-01), [SYN-F3-02](#syn-f3-02).

| Planning element | Illustrative implementation |
|---|---|
| Accountable role | Assessment lead with one delivery practitioner from each group; platform owners retain their local implementation decisions. |
| Days 0–30 | Agree plain-language labels for the three outcome checks, where evidence is found, and what counts as preparation time. Document workload differences that could affect comparisons. |
| Days 31–60 | Observe up to five eligible normal deliveries per group using existing records. Record case type and actual preparation time where available; report missing observations rather than estimate them. |
| Days 61–90 | Review outcome exceptions and effort distributions with local practitioners. Recommend a specific process improvement only if a comparable measured gap emerges; otherwise retain the current approaches. |
| Effort assumption | 3–6 combined person-days, assuming existing evidence can be located without instrumentation or a platform change. A need for either requires revised effort assumptions. |
| Measures | Evidence retrievable for each agreed check / eligible cases examined; observed preparation-time range with case counts and context; missing observations listed separately. |
| Proposed completion test | A reader can trace each assessed outcome to its local evidence and see where observations are absent or incomparable. No technology adoption or migration is a completion condition. |

**No inferred business case:** An unmeasured difference in automation does not establish labor savings. A small observed preparation-time difference would still need workload and transition-cost analysis before supporting a business case. No vendor evaluation or standardization recommendation is made here.

## Cross-example editorial check

- Each report paragraph names its scope, connects to finding IDs, and preserves material exceptions or dissent.
- Shared evidence is counted once; group reach is recorded separately. Prepared cases, live events, individual checks and participant accounts retain their own denominators.
- Missing records stay unknown. A documented timing departure stays distinct from a permitted exception and from an uncorroborated explanation.
- Common outcomes do not require common technology. Recommendations answer the supported finding rather than smuggling in a preferred tool or organizational redesign.
- Recommendation owners are role suggestions, effort is an assumption, and day ranges are a planning aid. None represents an actual assignment, scheduled event or approved work.
- All content remains fictional. Replace the evidence cards with authorized real evidence and reassess every finding before using this structure in an actual report.
