# Delivery Scope — Comprehensive AI Use and Governance Policy

## Commercial posture

Token Junkie Labs can provide a bounded AI-governance consulting workstream built around evidence-backed inventory, risk tiering, policy drafting, vendor/incident controls, and staff enablement. Any final engagement must be based on the District's executed contract and actual stakeholder/technology environment.

The District remains the authority for policy adoption, operational changes, legal interpretations, records decisions, procurement, cybersecurity response, and wastewater-process control.

## Six-month delivery model

### Phase 1 — Discovery and shadow-AI inventory

- kickoff with executive, IT/security, legal/records, HR, communications, engineering/operations, procurement, and representative end users;
- inventory sanctioned and unsanctioned GenAI / Operational AI use cases;
- capture system owner, lifecycle, automation level, data classes, impact areas, vendor/connectivity facts, public-facing output, and operational-state capability;
- map known contracts, existing acceptable-use/security/records policies, and current AI literacy;
- distinguish administrative GenAI from models or automation affecting treatment, infrastructure, safety, cybersecurity, or plant upgrades.

Outputs: inventory register, interview log, evidence map, shadow-AI findings, open-question register.

### Phase 2 — Risk taxonomy and control baseline

- apply deterministic inherent-risk tiers as a starting point for human review;
- define mandatory controls by risk tier and additional controls triggered by vendor, network, public-record, public-output, sensitive-data, and OT facts;
- reconcile the control model with District security, records, procurement, HR, and operational practices;
- identify control gaps without representing a missing control as an automatic shutdown/approval decision.

Outputs: risk taxonomy, control matrix, system-by-system review packet, control-gap backlog.

### Phase 3 — Policy and standards drafting

Draft a comprehensive policy covering at minimum:

- permitted / conditional / prohibited use patterns;
- roles and decision rights;
- GenAI employee-use standards;
- Operational-AI engineering and change-control expectations;
- data classification, sovereignty, input/output handling, and retention;
- public-record and transparency workflow;
- human oversight and output validation;
- logging, monitoring, model/vendor change management;
- incident reporting/escalation;
- third-party/vendor due diligence and contract clauses;
- procurement intake;
- exception and periodic-review mechanisms.

Outputs: draft policy, control standard, exception workflow, governance RACI.

### Phase 4 — Vendor, procurement, and incident framework

- AI vendor intake questionnaire and evidence checklist;
- data-residency/subprocessor/model-training-use review;
- contractual control prompts for retention, auditability, security, model changes, incident notice, exit/export, and records access;
- risk-tier-specific procurement gates;
- incident taxonomy separating GenAI information events from operational/OT/safety events;
- tabletop scenarios with human incident-command boundaries.

Outputs: vendor checklist, procurement decision worksheet, incident playbook, tabletop package.

### Phase 5 — Training and change enablement

- role-based training for general staff, managers, IT/security, records/legal/procurement, communications, and operations/engineering;
- safe prompt/data handling and verification practices;
- public-record implications;
- recognizing shadow AI and reporting concerns;
- higher-risk Operational-AI change/rollback/human-oversight expectations;
- manager discussion guides and quick-reference materials.

Outputs: training deck, facilitator notes, role-specific quick guides, knowledge checks.

### Phase 6 — Validation and adoption package

- stakeholder review and comment resolution;
- final inventory/control evidence refresh;
- crosswalk draft policy to findings and control matrix;
- adoption-ready final documents;
- implementation backlog with owners/sequence;
- recommended review cadence and measurable governance KPIs.

Outputs: final policy package, implementation roadmap, training handoff, governance metrics.

## Inputs needed from the District after award

1. Existing information-security, acceptable-use, public-records/retention, procurement, HR, communications, incident-response, and change-management policies.
2. Current/planned AI-enabled systems and vendor contracts.
3. Named stakeholders across administrative and wastewater/engineering functions.
4. OT/SCADA boundary description at an appropriate non-sensitive level.
5. Records classification and retention requirements from District counsel/records authority.
6. Current cybersecurity and vendor-review process.
7. Any planned plant-upgrade AI/analytics use cases.
8. Preferred policy approval path and board/management governance cadence.

## Explicit non-authorities

This package does not:

- make a Wisconsin public-records or other legal conclusion;
- determine that a system is legally permissible/prohibited;
- authorize or reject procurement;
- direct a production/OT change or shutdown;
- perform incident command;
- access District networks, SCADA/OT systems, vendor consoles, or confidential datasets;
- submit the RFP response or bind the District or Token Junkie Labs to contract terms;
- claim award, buyer acceptance, or revenue.

Those actions require the appropriate human/contractual authority and evidence outside this repository.
