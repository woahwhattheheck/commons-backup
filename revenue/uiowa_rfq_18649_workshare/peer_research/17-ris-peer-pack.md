## Decision-useful summary

Use these examples to prepare a service-boundary conversation and select concrete delivery records, not to assign a maturity score. The most useful comparison is the behavior of a research-administration workflow: a request crosses organizational boundaries, changes state, reaches a sponsor or finance system, and needs a named route when something goes wrong. Four complementary institutional examples are included; they are a purposive public-evidence sample, not a representative cohort or a ranking.

The selected public descriptions separate proposal/award administration from broader research portfolios. Michigan and Penn describe additional regulatory modules; Penn State also describes research operations. That does **not** establish that Iowa RIS owns clinical systems, laboratory systems, research computing, or those peers' products. Establish Iowa's actual boundary first. [UM-01](17-source-register.md#um-01), [PS-01](17-source-register.md#ps-01), [PN-01](17-source-register.md#pn-01).

**Three useful assessment moves, proposed by this analyst:** trace one ordinary request and one exception across confirmed local interfaces; compare the intended change process with the latest applicable release record; and separate a planned transition date from evidence that retirement and continuity actually occurred. These are interview design choices, not requirements imposed by the peer institutions.

**Actual comparison boundary:** eResearch Proposal Management (eRPM), specifically proposal routing, unfunded agreements, and award processing. The service page also mentions clinical-trial routing; this pack does not import that activity into Iowa's scope. The broader replacement project covers four product lines. **Published practice:** eRPM describes leadership shared among research leadership, sponsored projects, a research-administration council, and ITS, with campus input. The replacement team's published remit adds cross-functional governance and end-user participation. These are institutional role descriptions, not proof of review quality or decision speed. [UM-01](17-source-register.md#um-01), [UM-03](17-source-register.md#um-03).

**Dated change example:** the **August 31, 2026** SF-424 10.10.2 note reports a vendor update, including a changed federal assurance form. This is a concrete example of an external form dependency appearing in a local release record; it supplies neither a test result nor a defect-rate estimate. [UM-04](17-source-register.md#um-04).

**Transition context:** the replacement page says discovery concluded in **fall 2025** and the RFP phase began in **winter 2026**, prompted by the current vendor's support discontinuation. Treat the stated future transition as a plan, not a completed replacement or measured continuity result. The page's summarized peer advice is second-hand context; it is not counted here as additional independently researched institutions. [UM-02](17-source-register.md#um-02).

**Proposed transfer:** ask how research-business owners, developers, support, and dependent services agree on an externally driven change. Request a redacted change record, the applicable form/version, acceptance criteria, and the disposition of one exception. Ask separately about remaining support lifetime and transition evidence.

## P2 — Penn State: portfolio coordination and a staged entry-point transition

**Institution and issuer:** The Pennsylvania State University, Office of Research Information Systems and Solutions (ORISS), plus its public service-management knowledge base.  
**Actual comparison boundary:** sponsored-activity tools and the SIMS Central entry point. The wider ORISS remit includes other compliance, data, and research-operation services that are not automatically Iowa RIS responsibilities. [PS-01](17-source-register.md#ps-01).

**Published practice:** ORISS connects business analysis, development, data work, and operations, and names research administrators, investigators, colleges, university IT, information security, and external partners among its collaborators. Its sponsored-activity inventory distinguishes budgeting, submissions, approvals, agreements, documents, and workflow support. This is a portfolio/coordination description, not an architecture diagram or evidence that all interfaces share one implementation. [PS-01](17-source-register.md#ps-01).

**Dated transition example:** implementation article **KB0021580 v2.0**, visibly revised **December 4, 2025**, sets out a **September 16, 2025** go-live, a transition through **March 31, 2026**, and a planned retirement date. The document describes switching between old and new entry points during transition. Its future-tense language is retained as a plan, not proof that the retirement happened on schedule. [PS-03](17-source-register.md#ps-03).

**Later procedure:** **KB0020665 v16.0**, with a visible **September 5, 2026** version-history stamp, describes SIMS Central as implemented in fall 2025. It documents user announcements and enhancement/report request routes. Its footer also renders a relative modification time; use the explicit version stamp as a displayed metadata value, not an independently verified last-change timestamp. [PS-02](17-source-register.md#ps-02).

**Proposed transfer:** examine whether a change to a shared entry point preserves the ability to complete an existing business task. Ask who owns user communications, exceptions, retirement criteria, and proof that the old route is no longer required. Sample a request through the published support process, not just the presence of a menu.

**Non-comparable features:** an entry-point migration is not evidence of a replacement of all underlying systems. The published transition window is not a universal required duration. No realized downtime, retirement completion, support-volume improvement, or staff-size benchmark was established. **Matrix:** Q08–Q12, Q14.

## P3 — Washington: expose integration outcomes to operational owners

**Institution and issuer:** University of Washington, UW Research.  
**Actual comparison boundary:** SAGE proposal, award, budget, advance, and subaward administration, with the selected award-to-finance interface. The general service page describes training and support resources; those descriptions do not establish adoption or learning outcomes. [UW-01](17-source-register.md#uw-01).

**Dated change example:** the **September 8, 2026** release note says award-setup and modification requests in SAGE Central will show more integration success/failure detail to support GCA follow-up in Workday. The note describes the intended operational help, not a measured reduction in reconciliation time or errors. [UW-02](17-source-register.md#uw-02).

**Deliberate boundary:** the same release note also discusses an occupational-health transition. That material is excluded from the research-administration comparison; sharing a release-note page is not evidence of identical service ownership or of Iowa RIS clinical scope. [UW-02](17-source-register.md#uw-02).

**Proposed transfer:** trace an award or analogous confirmed local transaction across its interface. Ask what distinguishes accepted, pending, failed, manually corrected, and reconciled outcomes; who receives the exception; and what evidence prevents a retry or correction from being mistaken for a second successful transaction. Use redacted records or a synthetic walk-through, without exercising any external production system.

**Non-comparable features:** a named finance product and a specific central accounting role are peer context, not recommendations for Iowa's architecture. A useful operator message is not itself proof of complete observability, safe retry behavior, or effective recovery. **Matrix:** Q06–Q07, Q19.

## P4 — Pennsylvania: distinguish lifecycle modules, financial views, and support boundaries

**Institution and issuer:** University of Pennsylvania, Office of the Vice Provost for Research. This is **not Penn State**.  
**Actual comparison boundary:** PennERA proposal development/tracking and the separate Grant Financial Management (GFM) description. PennERA also lists protocol-related modules; their inclusion is not an assumption about Iowa RIS. Both selected pages are visibly undated. **Published practice:** PennERA distinguishes preparing/routing proposals from collecting application/award information. The separate GFM page describes budgets, balances, and user projections, a campus user forum, and vendor-provided support. These pages identify different user tasks and support surfaces; they do **not** document a technical PennERA-to-GFM integration, data freshness, or a successful migration. [PN-01](17-source-register.md#pn-01), [PN-02](17-source-register.md#pn-02).

**Proposed transfer:** ask users and maintainers to identify the authoritative record for a locally confirmed business fact, the meaning of derived projections, and who resolves inconsistent views. **Non-comparable features:** the selected evidence is service description only; no dated implementation outcome is asserted. Penn's modules, providers, organizational structure, and financial-data semantics are not an Iowa inventory. **Matrix:** Q01–Q02, Q15, Q17.

The interviewer should select appropriate participants within the engagement's agreed sampling and scheduling process; these questions create no appointments or attendance commitments.

**Suggested initial evidence request, subject to confirmed scope:** a service ownership map; two redacted request/change histories (ordinary and exception); the relevant interface/status definitions; one release or transition record; and one current support/runbook example. Ask for task evidence before requesting whole databases or sensitive research content. Actual records belong outside this public repository.
