## 3. What this says about the price points

- Every consumer anchor is a charm price ($27, $47, $97, $695, $997). The consumer tiers can follow ($19, $49, $99, $199) without looking cheap; the high tiers should stay round ($1,000, $10,000) because their anchors ($5,800, $9,500, $10,350) read as considered numbers and a round price signals the classier register.
- The $200 tier has the most crowded anchor band ($695–$1,095 memberships and coaching) and the biggest "built, not taught" gap to exploit. Lead the DESK ad with "already built".
- The $1,000 tier's real competitor is not a course, it is the franchise fee. "No royalty" is the two words that separate the pack from every franchise ad the buyer has seen, and the words survive every platform's review.
- The $10,000 tier has one true analogue (Business Blueprints at $9,500). Its door should read like theirs: application, conversation, build window, one client at a time.

## 4. Footnote for counsel (one line)

"No royalty" is only true if the owner's percentage/partial-ownership decision ends up as something other than an ongoing royalty; if a term royalty is chosen, the line becomes "no franchise fee".
