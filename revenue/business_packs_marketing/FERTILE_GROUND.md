## 1. The three layers (footnote for counsel)

Three layers apply to every campaign. Only the third varies by state.

1. **Federal, everywhere:** the FTC Act (no deceptive claims), the Business Opportunity Rule (16 CFR 437) and its earnings-claim definition, the Franchise Rule (16 CFR 436), CAN-SPAM, TCPA. No state makes these lighter. The copy rule "prices yes, earnings never" does not relax in any zip code.
2. **Platform policy, everywhere:** X, TikTok, Meta, Google and Rumble apply the same ad review in Wyoming as in California, and their automated review reads the landing page. No geo setting changes that.
3. **State overlays, which do vary:** business-opportunity / seller-assisted-marketing-plan registration and disclosure laws; franchise registration; consumer-privacy laws and their thresholds; the strength and damages of each state's unfair-and-deceptive-practices (UDAP) statute; sweepstakes and mystery-box rules; state telemarketing rules. **This is the layer geo-sequencing can sidestep**, and for the $1,000 and $10,000 tiers it is most of the compliance cost.

One more line that does not move: tjlabs' **own home-state law follows the seller** to every buyer. Whatever state tjlabs is organized and operating in applies to tjlabs' conduct regardless of where the ad runs. This seat did not determine that state; counsel starts there.

## 2. The overlays, state by state

### 2a. Business-opportunity / seller-assisted-marketing-plan laws (the one that bites $1,000 and $10,000)

States with a business-opportunity statute (about 25): **Alabama, Alaska, California, Connecticut, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Michigan, Nebraska, New Hampshire, North Carolina, Ohio, Oklahoma, South Carolina, South Dakota, Texas, Utah, Virginia, Washington** ([franchise.law overview](https://franchise.law/blog/what-are-business-opportunity-laws/), [Kanouse FTC-vs-state summary](https://www.kanouse.com/sites/default/files/FTC_Business_Opportunity_Rule_and_State_Business_Opportunity_Laws.pdf), [Oklahoma Business Opportunity Sales Act](https://securities.ok.gov/Act-Rules/Busopp.asp)) `(secondary lists; Oklahoma from the regulator)`.

They differ in two ways that matter to us:

- **Threshold.** Connecticut applies from **$200** initial payment; Alaska from **$250**; California's SAMP and Texas's Act from **$500** (California caps at $50,000) ([CT DOB guidelines](https://portal.ct.gov/DOB/Business-Opportunities/Bus-Opps/Guidelines-for-Business-Opportunity-Registration-in-Connecticut), [Cal. Civ. Code §1812.201](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-2-7/section-1812-201/), [Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §51.003](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._bus._and_com._code_section_51.003)). Consequence: the $20, $50 and $100 tiers are under every threshold found; the **$200 DESK tier meets Connecticut's $200 line** (price at $199 or exclude CT); the $1,000 and $10,000 tiers exceed every threshold.
- **Trigger.** Texas requires **both** an earnings representation (purchaser will earn more than the price) **and** one of location / marketing program / buy-back; a pack sold with no earnings claim is outside the Texas definition even though its runbook is a "marketing program" ([Tex. §51.003](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._bus._and_com._code_section_51.003)). California and Connecticut trigger on **any one** of several representations, and "the seller will provide a sales or marketing program" is one of them ([CA AG SAMP](https://www.oag.ca.gov/consumers/general/samp), [CT OLR report](https://www.cga.ct.gov/PS98/rpt/olr/htm/98-R-0451.htm)); a pack with a runbook is a marketing program, so those states are triggered by the product itself. Registration where triggered means a filed disclosure document, fees ($400 in Connecticut; $150 in Kentucky), waiting or cancellation periods, and in Texas a $25,000 bond, trust account or letter of credit when certain representations are made ([Texas SOS Form 2701](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/statdoc/forms/2701.pdf)) `(secondary)`.

**First-cut rule for the $1,000 and $10,000 tiers:** exclude all ~25 until counsel sorts them into "triggered by the marketing program alone" (exclude until registered) and "triggered only with an earnings claim" (fertile as long as the copy rule holds). That sort is the single highest-value legal hour on this file, because Texas, Florida and Georgia alone hold roughly a quarter of all US business formations (§3).

### 2b. Franchise registration and filing

Thirteen registration states (**CA, HI, IL, IN, MD, MI, MN, NY, ND, RI, VA, WA, WI**) and nine filing states (**CT, FL, KY, NE, NC, SC, SD, TX, UT**) ([FMS franchise guide](https://www.fmsfranchise.com/learn/resources/state-guidelines/), [Internicola registration states](https://www.franchiselawsolutions.com/learn/franchise-compliance/the-franchise-registration-states)). Relevant **only** if a pack operates under a tjlabs trademark (LAW_AND_POLICY_FLAGS §8a). The factory law's own-brand-per-instance keeps this layer off entirely; keep it that way and this table stays irrelevant.

### 2c. Consumer-privacy laws — mostly not applicable to a seller of tjlabs' size

Twenty comprehensive laws in effect in 2026 (**CA, VA, CO, CT, UT, TX, OR, MT, FL, DE, IA, NE, NH, NJ, TN, MN, MD, IN, KY, RI**; OK, AL, LA, VT enacted for 2027–28) ([MultiState 2026](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/2/4/all-of-the-comprehensive-privacy-laws-that-take-effect-in-2026), [Clym comparison](https://www.clym.io/blog/us-privacy-law-comparison-map)). Most apply only above ~100,000 consumers' data a year or large revenue; Texas and Nebraska exempt SBA-defined small businesses; Utah needs $25M revenue plus a count ([zerodaylaw applicability](https://www.zerodaylaw.com/blog/us-state-privacy-acts), [nerdstack small-business view](https://www.thenerdstack.com/posts/state-privacy-laws-small-business-websites)) `(secondary)`. **Exceptions that reach small sellers:** Delaware, Montana and Nevada have **no minimum threshold**; Connecticut and Rhode Island dropped to 35,000 consumers and Connecticut applies at **any volume to a business that sells personal data** — and a pixel "share" for cross-context advertising can be a "sale" ([zerodaylaw](https://www.zerodaylaw.com/blog/us-state-privacy-acts)). California's law applies at $26.6M revenue, 100k consumers, or 50% of revenue from selling/sharing data.

Consequence: the privacy notice and "Do Not Sell or Share" link recommended in DATA_BUYING §6 stay as good practice, but the *legal* exposure for a seller of tjlabs' size concentrates in DE, MT, NV, CT and RI. Cheap to satisfy everywhere; do not let it drive geography.

### 2d. UDAP statute strength (how badly a mistake hurts)

NCLC's 50-state evaluation rates **Colorado, Oregon and South Dakota** as having the weakest substantive prohibitions, and **Indiana and Arizona** as weak (Arizona reaches deception but not unfairness) ([NCLC 2018 report](https://filearchive.nclc.org/udap/udap-report.pdf), [NCLC state summaries](https://www.nclc.org/resources/appendix-a-capsule-summaries-of-strengths-and-weaknesses-of-each-states-udap-statute/)). Commonly cited strong statutes with private treble or statutory damages include Massachusetts (93A), New Jersey (CFA), Washington (CPA), California (UCL/CLRA), Illinois (CFA) and New York (GBL 349) `(secondary; verify against NCLC appendix)`. This layer matters only if copy goes wrong; with the copy rule held it is a tiebreaker, not a gate.

### 2e. Sweepstakes / mystery-box strictness

Washington and Idaho are cited as blocking probability-draw consumer mystery boxes ([tech-insider state guide](https://tech-insider.org/mystery-boxes/are-mystery-boxes-legal-in-the-usa/)) `(secondary)`. Moot while the "nuts" stay off ads and doors, which the factory law and LAW §4 already require.

### 2f. State telemarketing ("mini-TCPA") laws

Florida, Oklahoma, Washington and Maryland are commonly cited as the strictest for texts and calls `(secondary; verify)`. Moot while no campaign texts or calls purchased numbers (DATA_BUYING §2).

## 3. Where the buyers are

2025 business formations by state: Florida ~665,700, Texas ~449,800, New York ~258,200, Georgia ~239,100; Florida, California, Texas, Delaware and New York together are ~40% of US formations; Wyoming and Delaware per-capita counts are inflated by non-resident LLC filings and do not reflect local buyers ([Registered Agents Inc. report](https://www.registeredagentsinc.com/business-filing-report/august-2025/), [Wolters Kluwer 2025](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/news/wolters-kluwer-finds-strong-2025-growth-in-us-business-formations), [Census BFS](https://www.census.gov/econ/bfs/index.html)) `(secondary summaries of Census data)`. No official state-level side-hustle rate exists; use formations, suburban density (yard verticals) and franchise-seeker volume as the proxies.

## 4. Fertile ground, by campaign (first cut; counsel classifies before launch)

| campaign | where it is fertile | exclusions to set in geo targeting | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **$20 / $50 / $100** consumer packs | all 50 states + DC | none for law; keep "nuts" off ads everywhere (WA, ID strictest) | under every business-opportunity threshold found; privacy laws do not reach a seller this size except DE/MT/NV/CT/RI notice hygiene |
| **$200 DESK** | 49 states | **Connecticut** unless priced at $199 (CT bizopp applies from $200 with a marketing program) | Connecticut's $200 line; Alaska's $250 line is above $200 |
| **$1,000 PLANT** | the ~25 non-bizopp states, ranked: **Arizona, Tennessee, Missouri, Colorado, Nevada, Pennsylvania, Wisconsin, Minnesota, Oregon**, then Kansas, Arkansas, Mississippi, New Mexico, West Virginia, Wyoming, Montana, Idaho, North Dakota, Hawaii, DC; **New York** for volume with its stronger AG and GBL 349 in mind; Massachusetts and New Jersey last (treble damages) | all ~25 bizopp states (**CA, TX, FL, GA, IL, WA, NC, OH, MI, VA, MD, IN, IA, KY, LA, ME, NE, NH, SC, SD, UT, AL, AK, CT, OK**) until counsel classifies triggers or tjlabs registers | registration cost and disclosure regimes are the compliance that "kills a small business"; the fertile 25 still hold roughly 45% of the US population |
| **$10,000 HEAVY** (lead-gen) | same fertile list; weight toward metro operators: Phoenix, Nashville, St. Louis/Kansas City, Denver, Las Vegas, Philadelphia/Pittsburgh, Milwaukee, Minneapolis, Portland | same ~25 until classified; the sales law's YES-first motion means ads only buy conversations, so a lead from an excluded state can still be answered "not yet in your state" | the buyer is an operator; metro density beats state population |

Ranking logic for the fertile list: no business-opportunity statute first; then no comprehensive privacy law or a high applicability threshold; then weaker UDAP damages exposure; then buyer density. Arizona sits at the top on every axis but density and has the Phoenix suburban belt for the yard verticals; Tennessee, Missouri, Colorado and Nevada follow; Pennsylvania and New York trade stronger consumer statutes for volume.

**What the sidestep costs:** four of the five largest formation states (Florida, California, Texas, Georgia) sit in the excluded set. If counsel confirms that the Texas-type definition (earnings representation required) leaves an earnings-free pack outside it, Texas and any states with the same structure move to the fertile column, and the picture changes materially. That classification is the first legal ask this file produces.

## 5. Mechanics

Every platform in the plan supports state-level inclusion and exclusion: X (location targeting to state/metro), TikTok (state), Meta (state, with exclusions), Google (state/metro, exclusions), Rumble Advertising Center (country and, per the platform docs, location targeting to verify at campaign setup). The door itself should read the buyer's state (the paperwork page needs it anyway, PAPERWORK.md §2) and, in an excluded state, show "not yet available in your state; join the waitlist" rather than the checkout — which also turns the exclusion into first-party demand data (the waitlist demand, `scout-demand-pack-door-waitlist-20260902-01`).
