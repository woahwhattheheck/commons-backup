## Opportunity

The County of San Diego is soliciting **RFP 13259 / BPM013259** for a Document Management System serving the Assessor/Recorder/County Clerk (ARCC) and Auditor & Controller (A&C).

The County-issued RFP text publicly mirrored from the solicitation states:

- posted / dated **August 25, 2026**;
- proposals due **September 22, 2026, prior to 3:00 p.m. San Diego time**;
- initial base period of **one year plus nine one-year options**;
- official solicitation custody is the County's **BuyNet** portal;
- required solution is a **cloud-hosted, browser-based Commercial Off-the-Shelf (COTS) Document Management System**;
- scope includes configuration, implementation, hosting, licensing, migration, integrations, testing/UAT, training, go-live and ongoing operations/support.

The RFP is intended to replace ApplicationXtender and related document-management applications with a centralized system for document capture, indexing, retrieval, workflow, retention, public submission, reporting and auditability.

## Direct-prime qualification blocker

This is not an honest greenfield custom-software prime opportunity for the current Commons/Titan stack by itself.

The RFP asks the Offeror to confirm whether it has **successfully implemented and deployed a cloud-hosted, browser-based COTS DMS for a public-sector or government environment of similar scope and complexity**. It also asks for **at least three references from the last five years for projects of similar scope and complexity**, including a reference supporting that comparable-government-DMS answer.

Until those facts are backed by owned evidence, a direct-prime response is **HOLD**. Do not convert unrelated AI/agent/software work into fake COTS DMS references.

## Why it is still worth pursuing

The implementation and migration work is technically adjacent to capabilities already being developed:

- API / system integration and connector work;
- data migration, transformation and validation;
- document metadata/indexing and search workflows;
- human review, audit/evidence and provenance controls;
- public-facing forms/workflow integration;
- test planning, requirements traceability and acceptance evidence;
- workflow automation and optional AI-assisted features.

The stronger commercial route is therefore **partner-first**: pair with an established DMS product/OEM or public-sector implementation prime that owns the mandatory COTS product history and customer references, with Commons/Titan-style engineering positioned as a bounded implementation, migration, integration, testing, workflow or AI-enablement component.

## Material solicitation requirements recovered

### Technical / delivery

The RFP requires a complete production-ready DMS rather than a prototype. The proposed solution is expected to cover:

- centralized secure document storage, metadata/indexing and retrieval;
- capture via electronic upload, scanning, import services and integrations;
- viewing, printing, exporting, email, annotation, redaction, page management, version control and audit logging;
- configurable review/approval/routing workflows, notifications, electronic forms/signatures, correspondence templates, retention, disposition and legal holds;
- standard/custom/ad-hoc/administrative reporting;
- an approved public electronic-submission portal;
- migration from legacy repositories and metadata;
- integration with County applications and third-party systems;
- system, migration, regression and user-acceptance testing;
- training, cutover, go-live and ongoing operations/maintenance/support.

The proposal must describe a detailed implementation schedule, dependencies, County/Offeror responsibilities, migration, integration/testing, UAT, training, cutover and transition to ongoing support. If required functionality is deferred, the Offeror must identify the requirement, delivery timing, separate cost where applicable and operational impact.

### Experience / capacity

The County evaluates:

- enterprise DMS experience of similar scope;
- successful comparable cloud/browser COTS DMS implementations in public-sector/government or similarly regulated settings;
- favorable references;
- staffing/capacity;
- financial information supporting stability throughout the contract term.

### Security / compliance

The companion County IT-requirements workbook publicly indexed with the RFP includes substantial security and operational requirements. Before any bid, recover the authoritative workbook and verify every line item. Publicly surfaced requirements include controls around SSO, privacy, encryption, endpoint security, audit logging, accessibility, SOC reporting, hosting/data geography and support operations.

Do not claim compliance from a summary. Bind the actual workbook and answer each requirement with product/OEM evidence.

### Proposal / commercial rules

Recovered RFP text also establishes:

- electronic submission through BuyNet as the primary path;
- handwritten authorized signatures on required signature pages; digital/electronic signatures are not accepted for those pages;
- executive summary capped at five pages;
- technical, experience/capacity, price and terms/compliance evaluation, in descending importance as stated in the RFP;
- pricing segregated into the Cost/Price exhibit;
- potential demonstrations, clarifications, revised proposals and BAFOs;
- a Small-Local Business evaluated price preference of 15% or $150,000, whichever is less, for qualified firms;
- County public-records rules and special handling for properly asserted confidential/proprietary material.
