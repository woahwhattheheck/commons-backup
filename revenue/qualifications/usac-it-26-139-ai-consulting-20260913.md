## Direct-prime versus partner-first posture

### Direct prime can be considered only if

- the actual bidder can satisfy every mandatory experience/reference threshold;
- key personnel are genuinely available through the required performance period;
- regulated/federal-oversight experience requirements are met or are not mandatory;
- all legal/entity/insurance/confidentiality terms are acceptable;
- the bidder can credibly execute executive-facing discovery, workshops, governance design and roadmap delivery, not merely software engineering;
- the bid-sheet economics support a positive margin;
- all proposal representations can be backed by source evidence and an authorized signer.

### Partner-first is favored if

Technical AI/agent expertise is strong but one or more prime-contractor requirements are not yet evidenced — especially enterprise AI-governance references, management-consulting delivery history, regulated-industry credentials, key-personnel resumes, insurance, or contracting infrastructure.

A suitable prime could own formal strategy/change-management/compliance delivery while Commons/Titan expertise contributes:

- agentic-AI architecture and risk analysis;
- technical governance controls;
- evaluation / assurance design;
- AI lifecycle guardrails;
- pilot option analysis;
- technical feasibility and implementation sequencing.

The RFP must first confirm subcontracting / teaming rules and any required disclosure or approval.

### NO-BID if

Any mandatory requirement cannot be truthfully satisfied by the closing date, including:

- minimum comparable references / past-performance history;
- mandatory regulated-domain experience;
- mandatory key-personnel qualifications;
- bidder legal/entity requirements;
- required insurance;
- unacceptable confidentiality / IP / liability clauses;
- impossible performance location or schedule;
- proposal requirements that cannot be completed with adequate review margin;
- economics that cannot support the fixed-price risk.
