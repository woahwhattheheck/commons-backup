Stakeholder involvement will be structured so evidence collection, interpretation, and recommendation development remain transparent to the people who operate and govern the assessed services.

At kickoff, confirm the engagement sponsor, day-to-day University contact, ESS/RIS/IAM group leads, evidence custodians, and the interview roster. For each group, interview a mix of leadership, managers, and individual contributors representing different roles in the delivery lifecycle. Use leadership interviews to establish objectives, governance, and decision context; practitioner interviews and artifacts to test how work is actually performed; and group-lead checkpoints to resolve scope or evidence gaps.

During analysis, distinguish group-specific findings from department-level themes rather than forcing different stacks into one operating model. Materially concerning findings should be surfaced privately to the day-to-day contact and sponsor before publication, while the final assessment remains transparent to participating groups. Draft recommendations should be reviewed for factual accuracy, dependencies, adoption burden, and implementation practicality without allowing unsupported feedback to erase evidence-backed limitations.

Recommended informed/involved groups are the CIO/engagement sponsor, AIS day-to-day contact, ESS/RIS/IAM leadership, representative software/security/operations practitioners, relevant policy or data owners when an issue crosses domains, and any University stakeholders responsible for implementing accepted roadmap actions. The rationale is to combine executive context with direct workflow evidence and implementation ownership.
