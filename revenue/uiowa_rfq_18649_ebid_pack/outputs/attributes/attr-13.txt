Issues that warrant explicit attention in addition to the named assessment domains include:

• Evidence quality and comparability: distinguish stated policy from demonstrated practice, record source recency and scope, and avoid generalizing a single service or team sample.
• Cross-group consistency versus healthy variation: determine where ESS, RIS, and IAM need common guardrails and where different stacks or operating contexts justify different implementation patterns.
• Recovery evidence: separate successful backup jobs from demonstrated restoration, dependency recovery, and verified business-function recovery; where objectives are supplied, compare observed restoration evidence to the stated RPO/RTO.
• Delivery measurement quality: establish service-scoped definitions and denominators before interpreting lead time, deployment frequency, failed-deployment recovery time, change fail rate, or deployment rework rate.
• Test-data and sensitive-data handling: examine whether non-production workflows minimize exposure while preserving useful test coverage.
• Decision traceability: ensure recommendations can be traced to evidence and limitations, especially for security and AI-readiness topics where policy, tooling, and actual practice can diverge.
• Adoption capacity: prioritize recommendations with explicit implementation effort, ownership, dependencies, and practitioner impact so the roadmap is realistic for a multi-application central IT organization.

These are proposed assessment lenses, not pre-judged University findings.
