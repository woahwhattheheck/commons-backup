## Attribute 5 — Approach, Methodology, and Timeline

Status: **DRAFT_REQUIRES_OWNER**

Character count: **2129/4000** conservative (code points 2129, UTF-16 units 2129, UTF-8 bytes 2151)

~~~text
Proposed approach

• Kickoff and discovery (Week 1): confirm scope, source-handling rules, interview roster, evidence requests, and the working schedule.
• Interviews and artifact review (Weeks 2–4): engage ESS, RIS, and IAM leadership and practitioners; review authorized SDLC, security, deployment/operations, monitoring, incident, and AI-readiness evidence.
• Analysis and synthesis (Weeks 4–5): assess each group across software development, security, deployment/operations, and AI readiness; separate evidence confidence from maturity; identify cross-cutting strengths, gaps, and dependencies.
• Draft report and review (Weeks 6–7): provide traceable findings and phased practice/process recommendations for sponsor review, preserving unresolved evidence gaps rather than filling them with assumptions.
• Final report (Week 7–8): reconcile supported feedback, retain limitations and evidence provenance, and deliver the final written package.

Methodology

The assessment is evidence-led and practitioner-centered. Interviews establish context but do not automatically prove implementation. Findings are traced to observed evidence, scoped to the service/team population actually reviewed, and labeled for confidence, recency, representativeness, and corroboration. Materially conflicting evidence remains unresolved until bounded or reconciled. Recommendations are prioritized by expected impact and implementation complexity, phased into near-, medium-, and longer-term actions, and kept at the practice/process level rather than individual performance or specific commercial product/vendor selection.

Interview plan

For each participating group, use the University’s proposed mix of leadership, managers, and individual contributors. Begin with workflow and responsibility mapping, then use concrete examples and artifacts to test stated practice. Cross-group synthesis occurs only after per-group evidence is established. The integrated schedule targets the University’s six-to-eight-week window; exact interview times, onsite sessions, and kickoff date remain subject to contracting and University/prime coordination.
~~~

## Attribute 14 — Risk Assessment

Status: **DRAFT_REQUIRES_OWNER**

Character count: **1644/4000** conservative (code points 1644, UTF-16 units 1644, UTF-8 bytes 1660)

~~~text
Inherent project risks and planned controls include:

• Incomplete or uneven evidence across ESS, RIS, and IAM. Control: maintain an explicit evidence-request and gap register; use HOLD/unknown states instead of inferring maturity from missing material.
• Interview bias or self-reported practice being treated as proof. Control: corroborate material findings with artifacts or bounded examples and label evidence confidence.
• Comparing unlike teams or services. Control: assess service/group context first, define common measurement semantics, and only then synthesize cross-cutting themes.
• Scope drift into audit, individual performance evaluation, deep code review, or product/vendor selection. Control: preserve the RFQ scope boundary in interview plans, findings, and recommendation review.
• Sensitive information exposure. Control: use minimum-necessary evidence, authorized custody, no public-repository customer evidence, and the required confidentiality/destruction processes.
• Schedule compression or unavailable stakeholders. Control: identify critical interviews/artifacts early, surface blocked dependencies promptly, and keep the integrated six-to-eight-week schedule owned by the authorized engagement lead.
• Recommendations that are technically attractive but impractical to adopt. Control: phase actions by impact/complexity, identify skills/tooling/training dependencies, and review adoption burden with practitioners.
• Late source/amendment changes. Control: freeze source versions, track requirement deltas, and re-check the controlling eBid/RFQ materials before submission and before material assessment commitments.
~~~
