# Unary Kleene-star gaps and exact length navigation

For the declared unary dictionary
\[
L=\{0^{26},0^{51},0^{101},0^{126},0^{251},0^{276},0^{351},0^{551},0^{626}\},
\]
the complement of L* contains exactly 343 words. Its longest word has length 651, and every word of length at least 652 belongs to L*. A 26-row residue certificate and 234 edge inequalities establish this finite result. The complete reader output exports every missing length and retains huge-length queries without expanding the words.

## Source correction and established complexity

The PPL053 catalogue asks whether L* itself is infinite. The accessible text extraction of Shallit's bc4 printed slide 44 also renders L* without a complement bar. The screenshot response contained only an ImageDisplayed text placeholder, so no visual inspection of a bar was obtained. Mika and Szykuła's independent historical discussion (Section 1.1, printed page 2) explicitly identifies the question on Shallit's list as co-finiteness. This API addresses that independently documented Frobenius monoid question. A missing complement bar is a plausible explanation of the transcription discrepancy, not a visually verified fact. Literal infinitude of L* for an explicitly finite dictionary is elementary: one nonempty word yields arbitrarily long repetitions; if no such word exists, L*={epsilon}. It is not the intended historical question.

## Residue certificate

Let a_1<...<a_k be the positive lengths, g=gcd(a_1,...,a_k), b_i=a_i/g and m=b_1. The normalized generators have gcd one. On residues 0,...,m-1, put an edge
\[
r\longrightarrow(r+b_i)\bmod m
\]
of positive weight b_i. A path starting at zero is exactly a nonnegative generator combination with its residue recorded.

The residues generated by the b_i form all of Z/mZ: in a finite group the additive semigroup generated by them is a subgroup, and the gcd-one condition makes that subgroup the whole group. Every residue therefore has a finite shortest-path distance d_r. The array d is the classical Apéry residue-minimum representation of the normalized numerical semigroup.

A single deterministic Dijkstra pass computes the distances and parents. Ties in vertex selection use the least residue, and equal candidate distances do not replace an earlier parent. The certificate then records:

1. A nonnegative generator-multiplicity vector for each d_r, with exact reconstructed sum and residue.
2. Every edge slack d_r+b_i-d_s, where s=(r+b_i) mod m, all nonnegative.
3. d_0=0, parent records and the settled order.

The vectors prove that d_r is attainable. The inequalities telescope along every path from zero, proving that any attainable value of residue r is at least d_r. Together they certify exact minimality. This certificate generation and its exact arithmetic occurred in the one production construction, not in a separate replay pass or external proof checker.

Because m itself is a generator, the represented normalized values of residue r are exactly
\[
d_r,\ d_r+m,\ d_r+2m,\ldots .
\]
The missing values in that residue are r,r+m,...,d_r-m, when that range is nonempty. Hence
\[
F=\max_r d_r-m,\qquad C=F+1,\qquad
G=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}(d_r-r)/m.
\]
Here F is the normalized Frobenius number, C the conductor and G the number of normalized gaps. If m=1, F=-1, C=0 and G=0: every nonnegative integer is represented.

For the actual gcd-one input, max d_r=677, F=651, C=652 and G=343. Every residue row and every edge slack is retained, not a selected sample.

## Scaling and the infinite-complement case

An original length n is represented precisely when g divides n and n/g >= d_(n/g mod m). If g>1, all lengths not divisible by g are absent, so the complement is infinite. The remaining absent multiples are exactly g times the finite normalized gaps. In this case the API returns null for a global largest gap, global conductor and total gap count; it separately retains the finite normalized values.

openUnary(record,{dilation:'2'}) multiplies every original generator and g by two while retaining the same normalized graph. This follows directly from scaling nonnegative linear combinations and needs no new shortest-path calculation. The actual reader used that view: lengths {52,102,202,252,502,552,702,1102,1252} have infinite complement, comprising every odd length and 343 exceptional even lengths.

The dilated view is an explicitly declared new dictionary. It does not change or mutate the saved base certificate and is not a new result about the base language.

## Counting, ordering and compressed witnesses

For T>=0, put Q=floor(T/g). The number of represented lengths through T, including zero, is
\[
R(T)=\sum_r \max(0,\lfloor(Q-d_r)/m\rfloor+1).
\]
The gap count is T+1-R(T). For negative private prefix bounds both counts are zero. Subtracting two prefix counts gives exact inclusive interval counts.

Ranks are zero-based, with lengths in increasing numerical order. Represented rank zero is length zero. Missing rank zero is the smallest positive gap. Binary search over these monotone exact count formulas implements selection. The kth represented length is at most gm k because the first k+1 multiples of gm are represented. When g>1, the kth missing length is at most g(k+1), since the nonmultiples already provide enough gaps. For g=1, the finite search upper bound is F, and ranks at least G are rejected.

Each selection retains its final before/through counts, membership certificate and search-step count. Intermediate binary-search probes are query arithmetic, not a newly compiled table. The implementation does not pretend that huge ranges were enumerated.

A membership witness for n=g(d_r+tm) combines the saved multiplicities for d_r with t more copies of the smallest generator. It reports a vector and reconstructed total, rather than expanding a potentially enormous word. This is one valid decomposition, not necessarily the decomposition using the fewest dictionary words and not a count of all decompositions. Reordering the concatenated unary words does not produce a different word.

## API

~~~javascript
const { openUnary } = require('./unary_frobenius.cjs');
const saved = require('./unary_dictionary_certificate.json');
const reader = openUnary(saved.record);

reader.summary();
reader.membership('651');
reader.decompose('652'); // one word of length 626 and one of length 26
reader.selectMissing('342'); // final gap
const doubled = openUnary(saved.record, { dilation: '2' });
doubled.selectMissing('1000000000000000000000000000000');
~~~

| Method | Result |
|---|---|
| compileUnary(input) | New residue compiler; accepts an identified nonempty positive-length dictionary. |
| openUnary(record,options?) | Structurally loads the certificate, optionally with a positive integer dilation. |
| summary() | Generator lengths, gcd, finite/infinite distinction and normalized/global gap data. |
| membership(length) | Membership or an exact gcd/residue obstruction. |
| decompose(length) | One compressed generator vector, or the missing-length obstruction. |
| count(through) | Represented and missing nonnegative lengths through an inclusive endpoint. |
| interval(lo,hi) | Exact counts on a closed interval. |
| selectMissing(rank), selectRepresented(rank) | Ordered selection with final count-boundary evidence. |
| rankMissing(length), rankRepresented(length) | Rank in the requested family; reject the other family. |
| pageMissing(start,limit) | At most 256 complete gap-selection records. |
| gapRuns() | Every finite arithmetic-progression gap block, plus the nonmultiple predicate when needed. |
| pageRows(start,limit) | At most 256 saved residue records. |
| work(), snapshot() | Copies of query counters and the original saved record. |

Lengths, multiplicities and ranks use canonical unsigned decimal strings. The signed Frobenius value -1 occurs only in saved/summary metadata for a gap-free normalized semigroup. Indices and page sizes are safe integers. The compiler accepts at most 64 generators, each at most 100 decimal digits, normalized minimum at most 4096, and at most 262,144 residue/generator edges. Numeric query strings are capped at 1200 digits; a selection whose search bound exceeds that cap is rejected. Dilation is positive and at most 100 digits.

The implementation uses a simple O(m^2+mk) arithmetic-operation Dijkstra construction, plus the certificate rows. This is bounded by the numeric modulus, and is not a polynomial bound in its compressed bit length. Counting takes m exact terms per endpoint; selection adds logarithmically many endpoint counts in its numeric search bound. Arithmetic costs depend on integer sizes.

Reopening performs structural checks only. It trusts the saved arithmetic and identified provenance; it does not authenticate a hostile certificate or recheck its edge inequalities. All returned records are copies. Alternative inputs and larger resource branches were source-inspected, not exercised.
