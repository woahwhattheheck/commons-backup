# Exact divisibility placement and a complete residue period

This package finds the least inclusive width containing distinct labeled multiples of $1,\ldots,n$. Each completed result carries two concrete certificates: an assignment at the returned width and a Hall obstruction at the immediately preceding width.

The actual complete $n=11$ period establishes

$$
\max_{m\ge1} W(11,m)=20.
$$

The maximum occurs exactly when

$$
m\equiv100\pmod{110}
\quad\text{or}\quad
m\equiv12145,\ 15555\pmod{27720}.
$$

This covers every positive $m$ for the fixed value $n=11$, by periodicity. It is not an asymptotic result as $n$ grows. All 27,720 residue cases retain their assignment and preceding-width obstruction.

## 1. Primary conventions and attribution

Write $W(n,m)$ for the least positive integer $w$ such that there are pairwise distinct integers $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ satisfying

$$
m<a_i\le m+w,\qquad i\mid a_i\quad(1\le i\le n),
$$

where $n,m$ are positive integers. This is the right-inclusive two-variable convention in **Paul Erdős and Carl Pomerance**, [*Matching the natural numbers up to n with distinct multiples in another interval*](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/PDF/matching.pdf), *Proceedings A* 83(2) (1980), pp. 147–161. The paper calls this width $f(n,m)$; its one-variable $f(n)$ instead denotes the endpoint $n+W(n,n)$.

Unused target integers are allowed. The assignment need not increase with $i$, and it is not a permutation of the entire interval. For general $m$, target values may overlap numerically with the divisor labels; the two sides of the matching graph remain distinct vertex copies.

For integer parameters, an open-right interval requires width $W(n,m)+1$. This distinguishes the primary definition from the catalogue's open-right rendering. At $n=11$, the retained diagonal width is 14, the primary one-variable endpoint is 25, and the corresponding open-right integer width is 15.

The divergence subquestion of Erdős 711 already has a published solution. **Wouter van Doorn**, [*On the length of an interval that contains distinct multiples of the first n positive integers*](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/aa7/aa7.pdf), *Integers* 26 (2026), #A7, [DOI 10.5281/zenodo.18154085](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18154085), proves

$$
\max_m W(n,m)-W(n,n)>0.36\,n\log n/\log\log n
$$

for all sufficiently large $n$. That result settles the divergence part, not the separate uniform $n^{1+o(1)}$ upper-bound target. No explicit cutoff is inferred here, and this package makes no new general-bound, priority, or exhaustive-frontier claim.

The published $n=10$ example was not replayed. The present computation uses the new fixed-$n$ period stated above, preserving the original matching and periodicity attribution.

## 2. Matching model

For width $w$, use left vertices $1,\ldots,n$ and right vertices given by offsets $1,\ldots,w$. Connect divisor $i$ to offset $j$ exactly when

$$
i\mid m+j.
$$

A matching saturating all left vertices is precisely the required labeled injection. Offset $j$ represents the ordinary positive integer $m+j$.

Edges are generated by arithmetic progressions, without factoring $m+j$. For divisor $i$, the first positive matching offset is

$$
d_i=i-(m\bmod i)\in\{1,\ldots,i\},
$$

and subsequent offsets are $d_i+i,d_i+2i,\ldots$. A right-vertex arrival checks which progressions meet that position.

### Incremental maximum matching

The compiler adds right vertices in increasing offset order. It begins with the empty maximum matching. When offset $j$ arrives, a breadth-first alternating-path search starts at that new right vertex.

The maximum matching can increase by at most one. If a larger matching exists, the symmetric difference with the old matching contains an augmenting path. Such a path cannot lie entirely in the old graph, because the old matching was maximum. It must therefore use the newly added right vertex, which is exactly the search root. Flipping a found path increases the matching size by one; failing to find one preserves maximal size.

This gives an exact maximum matching after every completed arrival, rather than a greedy assignment whose failure might be inconclusive. Neighbor order is deterministic, using decreasing divisor labels; correctness does not depend on that order.

### Exact minimum from two certificates

Suppose the first full matching appears at width $w$. The preceding graph has maximum matching size $n-1$, because one new right vertex just increased it to $n$.

Starting from its unmatched left vertex, traverse all left-to-right edges and matched right-to-left edges in the graph of width $w-1$. Let $S$ be the reached left set and $N(S)$ its right neighbors. No reached right vertex is unmatched; that would provide an augmenting path in a maximum matching. Its matched mate is reached on the left. Conversely, every reached left vertex except the starting unmatched vertex is the mate of a reached right vertex. Hence

$$
|S|=|N(S)|+1.
$$

This is an explicit Hall obstruction: the divisors in $S$ have too few distinct available targets at width $w-1$. It also excludes every smaller width. Together with the assignment at $w$, it proves the returned minimum without requiring the reader to reproduce any intermediate matching search.

The compact record retains all assignment offsets, the full divisor set $S$, and the full neighbor-offset union $N(S)$ as bit masks. Its meaning is exact set membership, not a hash or probabilistic summary.

## 3. Complete periodic reduction

The original paper states that $W(n,m)$ depends only on $m$ modulo

$$
L(n)=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n).
$$

Indeed, every divisor label divides $L(n)$, so translating $m$ by a multiple of $L(n)$ leaves every offset edge unchanged. Distinct assigned offsets remain distinct, and their integer targets translate together. A single complete residue table therefore applies to every positive $m$.

For $n=11$, $L(11)=27720$. The compiler processed rows $r=0,\ldots,27719$, using the positive representative $m=27720+r$. Row $r$ applies to every positive $m\equiv r\pmod{27720}$. It does not assert that this is the smallest possible period.

The recorded maximum is both an upper and lower bound for all positive $m$ at this fixed $n$: every residue has a successful assignment of width at most 20, and the maximizing rows have Hall obstructions at width 19.

This finite-period argument supplies no uniform control as $n$ increases. The full-period compiler refuses periods beyond its stated bound.

## 4. Public interface

The dependency-free CommonJS module exports:

| Export | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `solveDivisibilityPlacement(request)` | Solve one positive $(n,m)$, returning an assignment and preceding-width Hall obstruction when completed. |
| `compileDivisibilityPlacementPeriod(request)` | Compute every residue of one bounded full period and retain a compact certificate for each row. |
| `assembleRetainedPlacementPeriod({manifest, shards})` | Assemble explicitly identified contiguous row shards without replaying matching. |
| `openRetainedPlacementPeriod({source_id, snapshot})` | Open a complete retained period as a stated source premise. |
| `PLACEMENT_LIMITS` | Input, work, width, period, and paging bounds. |

A single-point request contains `source_id`, `n`, and `m`, with optional `max_width` and `budget`. A period request contains `source_id` and `n`, with an optional shared work budget. Positive `m` accepts a BigInt, safe integer Number, or canonical decimal string. Unsafe Numbers, zero, negative values, and noncanonical decimal strings are rejected.

A retained period index exposes:

| Method | Result |
|---|---|
| `lookup(m)` | Exact width, assignment values/quotients, preceding-width Hall sets, and complete neighbor-progression descriptions for a positive integer $m$. |
| `maximizingResiduePage({start_index, limit})` | A page of the saved maximizing residue classes, in increasing order. |
| `describe()` | Period, exact summary, source label, and explicit premise/validation boundaries. |
| `snapshot()` | Detached JSON-compatible retained data. |

A lookup uses one residue row. It translates offsets, divides assigned integers by their known divisor labels to display quotients, and describes the obstruction's arithmetic progressions. It does not run a matching algorithm or recompute the union of obstruction neighbors.

### Result and stop states

A completed point result has status `EXACT_MINIMUM_WIDTH`. Its `width` uses $(m,m+W]$; `open_right_integer_width` is $W+1$.

If a point search reaches a width or work limit, it returns `RESOURCE_LIMIT` with the generated graph, current matching, last fully processed width, and stop reason. A graph generated through one additional right vertex may have an unfinished alternating search at that vertex; the reported maximum matching is explicitly bound to the last completed width.

A full period has status `EXACT_PERIODIC_TABLE`. If a shared budget stops it, `PARTIAL_PERIOD` retains all finished rows, the next residue, and the partial current point. It does not claim an exact maximum over the unfinished period. Complete-table loaders refuse partial status.

## 5. Shards, explicit records, and trust boundary

A row is

```text
[width, offsets_by_divisor_1_through_n, Hall_divisor_mask_hex, Hall_neighbor_mask_hex]
```

For either mask, bit $i-1$ represents the positive integer $i$. Divisor masks refer to labels; neighbor masks refer to offsets at the failed width. Hexadecimal strings have no prefix and no leading zeros, except the zero mask `"0"`.

The actual table is stored in two explicit JSON shards:

| File | Residues | Rows |
|---|---:|---:|
| `period_n11_rows_00000_13859.json` | 0–13,859 | 13,860 |
| `period_n11_rows_13860_27719.json` | 13,860–27,719 | 13,860 |

Each row occupies one readable JSON line. `period_n11_manifest.json` supplies the full period header, exact summary, ordered shard descriptors, source references, maximizing residues, and actual retained-index consumers.

The assembler requires every declared shard exactly once, matching its identifier, $n$, period, start residue, and row count. Coverage must be contiguous, ordered, and exactly complete. Missing, duplicated, inconsistent, or extra shards are refused.

These identifiers are labels, not content authentication. The loader checks row shapes, bounded widths, distinct in-range assignment offsets, mask encoding/ranges, and ordered maximizing residue labels. It does not recompute the lcm, verify assignment divisibility, form Hall neighbor unions, replay matching, or recompute the summary. All such mathematical content is an explicitly retained premise. Use an appropriate trusted, pinned repository version; the loader is not an independent certificate validator.

### Reuse without matching

```js
const api = require("./divisibility_placement.cjs");
const saved = require("./period_n11_manifest.json");
const shards = [
  require("./period_n11_rows_00000_13859.json"),
  require("./period_n11_rows_13860_27719.json")
];

const assembled = api.assembleRetainedPlacementPeriod({
  manifest: saved.period_manifest,
  shards
});
const index = api.openRetainedPlacementPeriod({
  source_id: "the pinned Commons n=11 manifest and row shards",
  snapshot: assembled.snapshot
});

const result = index.lookup(
  "277200000000000000000000000000000000000012145"
);
// width 20; six Hall divisors with only five available offsets at width 19
const maxima = index.maximizingResiduePage({start_index: 0, limit: 256});
// all 254 maximizing residue classes, next_index null
```

For connected V8, evaluate a previously retrieved module string with `new Function("module","exports", sourceText)` and supply previously retrieved JSON objects to the same exports. The module performs no network, filesystem, process, or provider operations. Source retrieval and pinning remain outside it.

## 6. Bounds

| Resource | Maximum |
|---|---:|
| Divisor labels $n$ | 128 |
| Decimal digits in positive input $m$ | 256 |
| Width explored by one point | 4,096 |
| Residues in a full period | 32,768 |
| Divisor-progression checks | 16,777,216 |
| Alternating-search edge visits | 33,554,432 |
| Maximizing residues in one page | 256 |

The optional work budget can reduce the two visit limits, never increase them. A standalone point defaults to width $\min(n^2,4096)$; the caller may specify another positive width within the hard cap.

The elementary bound $n^2$ is sufficient: an interval of that width contains at least $n$ multiples of every divisor $1,\ldots,n$, so the labels can be assigned greedily to distinct targets. Thus all periods admitted here have an adequate width limit; work limits can still interrupt a run. The implementation does not use this elementary bound as a claim of sharpness.

Period construction stops before exceeding 32,768 residues and raises `PlacementPeriodLimitError` with the first exceeding lcm step. No truncated residue sample is labeled a complete period. The period multiplication remains within safe integer arithmetic at the checked step.

Large target values and quotients use BigInt. Divisor labels, bounded offsets, row indices, lcm steps below the refusal bound, and visit counters use safe integer Numbers. Expanding a target near the input digit limit may add one output digit.

The width sum is 390,542, so the exact mean over one full period is $195271/13860$. The diagonal width $W(11,11)=14$ has primary endpoint 25. The fixed-$n$ maximum exceeds that diagonal width by 6.

All 27,720 rows contain eleven assignment offsets and a preceding-width Hall obstruction of deficiency one. The complete table, rather than only its histogram or extreme rows, is retained.

### All maximizing classes

The table yields 252 maximizing classes $r=100+110t$ for $0\le t<252$, and two additional classes $12145$ and $15555$ modulo 27,720. There are no other maximizing residues.

The two-label obstruction has an elementary general form. For $n\ge2$, suppose

$
m\equiv(n-1)^2\pmod{n(n-1)}.
$

The first positive multiple offsets for divisors $n-1$ and $n$ are both $n-1$: the previous offsets are respectively 0 and $-1$, while the next are $2n-2$ and $2n-1$. Thus, at width $2n-3$, these two labels have the single available target $m+n-1$. Hall's condition fails, giving $W(n,m)\ge2n-2$. This argument needs no primality assumption and concerns selected shifts $m$, not the diagonal $m=n$. It is included as an elementary explanation of the recorded residue family, without a claim of priority.

For the first congruence family, the lower bound is especially direct. If $m\equiv100\pmod{110}$, both divisor 10 and divisor 11 have the same sole available multiple $m+10$ within $(m,m+19]$. They cannot receive distinct targets, so $W(11,m)\ge20$. The complete table supplies the corresponding upper bound for every full-period residue in that family; a single representative assignment is not substituted for all of them.

The exceptional obstructions are:

| Residue modulo 27,720 | Hall divisors | Available offsets at width 19 |
|---:|---|---|
| 12,145 | $\{5,7,8,9,10,11\}$ | $\{5,7,10,14,15\}$ |
| 15,555 | $\{5,7,8,9,10,11\}$ | $\{5,6,10,13,15\}$ |

Each row has six demands and five available targets. Their assignments at width 20 are respectively

```text
[4,1,2,3,20,11,7,15,14,5,10]
[2,7,9,1,15,3,20,13,6,5,10]
```

These are offsets ordered by divisor label, not the target integers themselves. They translate to every positive $m$ in their respective full-period residue class.

The call took 834 ms in that observation, which is not a statistical benchmark. No original-paper example or earlier accepted table was rerun.

After sharding, a fresh connected V8 invocation assembled and opened the saved period. It looked up $m=27720\cdot10^{40}+100$ and $m=27720\cdot10^{40}+12145$, preserving both complete assignments, their integer quotients, and the two different Hall mechanisms. One page exported all 254 maximizing residues. This reuse performed no matching search or Hall-union reconstruction.

The period core and witness construction were exercised by the complete consumer; the standalone wrapper and resource-stop branches were not separately exercised in a synthetic suite. The guide's proofs and stated contracts explain the general behavior without relabeling that one finite run as full branch coverage.

## 8. What the result establishes

For fixed $n=11$, the complete certificates and periodicity establish the exact width for every positive $m$, the maximum 20, its complete residue classification, and the exact period average. This is stronger than a sample of starting offsets, and narrower than a theorem uniform in $n$.

The original authors' conventions, matching framework, periodicity, and later published divergence result remain credited. The finite computation and reusable API neither resolve the separate asymptotic questions nor claim an external computational record.
