# Universal inherited-coloring equations

This package delivers a complete symbolic equation system for **six labeled vertices and three declared colors** in the Krenn–Gu graph model. It stores 135 aggregate edge-weight variables, all 15 pairings of the vertices, all 729 coloring equations, and all 10,935 cubic monomials. Each of the three constant-color amplitudes has right-hand side one; the other 726 equations have right-hand side zero.

The implementation provides a compiler and a saved reader. The reader navigates equations and incidence lists, extracts derivatives and linear expansions, restricts channels to zero, maps vertex/global-color symmetries, and formats exact polynomial expressions. The delivered execution compiled one atlas and opened it once for 22 recorded queries. It did not choose numerical weights or solve the equations.

## Mathematical contract

Krenn, Gu and Soltész [1, Definitions 2.1–2.3 and Question 1] use a loopless undirected graph with ordered vertices, a complex weight on each edge, and a possibly different color at each endpoint. Each perfect matching induces a vertex coloring through its incident edges. Its contribution is the product of its edge weights; contributions inducing the same coloring are added coherently. Their target requires every declared constant coloring to have amplitude one and all other colorings to have amplitude zero. The multigraph formulation is explicit in [2, Section 1.1].

For vertices `0,...,n-1` and colors `0,...,d-1`, introduce a variable

$$
x_{uv}^{ab},\qquad 0\le u<v<n,\quad 0\le a,b<d.
$$

The superscripts identify the colors at `u` and `v` respectively. Let `M_n` be the set of pairings of all vertices. For a coloring $\alpha=(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_{n-1})$, define

$$
P_\alpha(x)=
\sum_{M\in M_n}
\prod_{\{u,v\}\in M,\ u<v}x_{uv}^{\alpha_u\alpha_v},
\qquad
b_\alpha=
\begin{cases}
1,&\alpha\text{ is constant},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

The stored equations are `P_alpha = b_alpha`. Their residual polynomials are `P_alpha - b_alpha`. The 10,935 count concerns the cubic amplitude monomials; residual form additionally has three constant terms equal to minus one.

Products are commutative, every amplitude-monomial coefficient is one, and variables are formal complex weight coordinates. There are no determinant/Pfaffian signs or complex conjugates in this amplitude sum. Cancellation becomes relevant when variables receive complex values. Counting formal terms does not establish whether a specialized amplitude vanishes.

### Why aggregate variables cover the multigraph model

For a supplied graph, let $E_{uv}^{ab}$ be its edges joining `u` to `v` with endpoint colors `a,b`. Set

$$
x_{uv}^{ab}=\sum_{e\in E_{uv}^{ab}}w_e.
$$

For a fixed pairing and coloring, a matching independently chooses one edge from each corresponding channel. Distributivity gives

$$
\prod_{\{u,v\}\in M}
\left(\sum_{e\in E_{uv}^{\alpha_u\alpha_v}}w_e\right)
=
\sum_{\text{one edge per channel}}\prod_e w_e.
$$

Summing this identity over pairings gives the graph's exact inherited-coloring amplitude. Empty channels have value zero. Conversely, any assignment of complex values to these coordinates can be represented by one edge per channel, omitting zero channels. Thus the equations describe every amplitude assignment available to this fixed vertex/color model.

This reduction preserves amplitudes. It can merge different original edge identities, alter edge-level matching counts, and remove structural feasibility when a group of parallel weights sums to zero. The atlas therefore makes no claim to preserve a particular graph's original edge records or matching multiplicities.

In particular, a common zero of the residual equations would supply the stated six-vertex, three-color graph. The saved atlas does not establish that such a common zero exists or fails to exist. Additional vertices, heralding conditions, and other parameter pairs are separate systems.

### Completeness of the monomial list

Pairings are generated by selecting the smallest remaining vertex, pairing it with each other remaining vertex in increasing order, and recursing. Every pairing has a unique first partner and a unique recursively determined suffix. Induction therefore gives every pairing exactly once.

There are

$$
|M_n|=(n-1)!!,\qquad
d^n\text{ colorings},\qquad
\binom n2d^2\text{ variables}.
$$

For a fixed coloring, each pairing produces a different monomial: changing a pairing changes an endpoint-pair channel. A monomial also uniquely fixes its inherited coloring, because each vertex occurs exactly once. Consequently, monomials from different colorings are distinct. Each monomial has degree `n/2` and is square-free in its variables.

At `n=6,d=3`, these counts give `15` pairings, `729` equations, `135` variables and `729*15 = 10,935` monomials. Each variable occurs in

$$
(n-3)!!\,d^{n-2}=3\cdot3^4=243
$$

monomials: its edge fixes two vertices and their colors, leaving a pairing and coloring of the other four. The incidence table therefore has `135*243 = 32,805` entries, also equal to `3*10,935`.

### Unit amplitudes and a normalization

All three constant-color equations are required. A zero polynomial or unused color cannot pass the target by vacuity.

The 2024 paper [2, Section 1.4] relates unit-weight GHZ graphs to a broader nonzero-amplitude formulation. For this atlas, a direct coordinate normalization is available: suppose constant-color amplitudes are $a_c\ne0$ and mixed amplitudes are zero. Divide each edge weight incident to vertex `0` by $a_c$, where `c` is that edge's color at vertex `0`. Every matching uses exactly one such edge, so

$$
P'_\alpha=P_\alpha/a_{\alpha_0}.
$$

The constant amplitudes become one and mixed zeros remain zero. This argument requires every declared $a_c$ to be nonzero. It is a mathematical observation about the model; no normalization or numerical weight evaluation was executed by this package.

## Record layout and stable identities

The JSON file is an evidence wrapper. Pass its `record` member to `openAtlas`. The record itself has six fields: `schema`, `source_id`, `model`, `core`, `construction` and `sessions`. Extra annotations belong outside that record.

| Core table | Row format | Ordering / meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `pairs` | `[u,v]` | All `u<v` pairs in lexicographic order |
| `variables` | `[u,v,a,b]` | Pair order, then color at `u`, then color at `v` |
| `pairings` | Array of `n/2` pair IDs | Increasing pair IDs within each row; rows in lexicographic order |
| `equations` | `[color_0,...,color_(n-1),rhs]` | All base-`d` color words in increasing numeric order |
| `terms` | Array of `n/2` variable IDs | Equation order, then pairing order |
| `incidence` | Increasing array of global term IDs | One complete containing-term list per variable |

All row IDs are zero-based. For `u<v`, the pair ID is

$$
p(u,v)=\frac{u(2n-u-1)}2+(v-u-1).
$$

The variable ID is `p(u,v)*d*d + a*d + b`. Its default printed symbol is `x` followed by that ID. Color words use vertex `0` as the most significant base-`d` digit:

$$
\operatorname{id}(\alpha)=\sum_{v=0}^{n-1}\alpha_vd^{n-1-v}.
$$

A term with equation ID `e` and pairing ID `p` has global ID `e*pairing_count+p`. The array stores its variable IDs; coefficient one and the equation/pairing IDs follow from this layout. Each term's variables are already increasing because its endpoint-pair IDs are increasing.

In the delivered atlas, constant colorings have equation IDs `0,364,728`. Coloring `[0,0,1,1,2,2]` has equation ID `44`. Its first term is global term `660`:

| Variable | Channel |
|---|---|
| `x0` | Vertices `0,1`, endpoint colors `0,0` |
| `x85` | Vertices `2,3`, endpoint colors `1,1` |
| `x134` | Vertices `4,5`, endpoint colors `2,2` |

That term is `x0*x85*x134`. Its pairing is `[[0,1],[2,3],[4,5]]`.

The `construction` member retains the exact compiler arguments and work counters. `sessions` begins empty in the compiler output. Each successful `openAtlas` appends one reader session to its own copied record. A session contains its ID, opening checks, ordered events and accumulated query work. Events store `reference: {session,event}`, method, actual arguments, result and work.

### What opening checks

Opening makes a detached copy of strict, bounded JSON before using it. It checks the exact model counts, canonical pair/variable identities, complete color keys and right-hand sides. It checks that the pairing rows are distinct valid vertex covers in canonical order and that each saved term uses the stated pairing with the stated endpoint colors.

These checks establish finite completeness through the counting argument above. There are exactly `(n-1)!!` possible pairings; a list with that many distinct valid pairings is complete. Every coloring appears once, and every pairing contributes its required term once. Incidence lists have the exact count of distinct valid containing terms. No second pairing enumeration or new monomial/incidence table is constructed during opening.

This is the meaning of `finite_atlas_complete: true`. It verifies the internal finite representation. It does not authenticate who produced the record, its claimed execution history, a particular physical graph, or a numerical solution. `source_origin_authenticated` remains `false`.

For prior sessions, opening checks references, method names, record shape and work-counter sums. It does not re-evaluate their query results. The delivered reader opened a record with no prior reader sessions; the final export containing 22 events was not opened again. Historical-query validation is therefore an unexecuted path for this delivery.

## Use the saved reader

```js
const { openAtlas } = require("./equation_atlas.cjs");
const evidence = require("./six_vertex_three_color_atlas.json");
const atlas = openAtlas(evidence.record);

const equation = atlas.lookupColoring({ colors: [0, 0, 1, 1, 2, 2] });
const terms = atlas.pageTerms({ equation_id: 44, offset: 0, limit: 64 });
const derivative = atlas.differentiate({ equation_id: 44, variables: [0] });
```

This example describes a future reader session. The delivered evidence already includes these types of operations in its actual session `0`. Documentation and publication did not run them again.

Every recorded query returns an event-shaped object with `reference`, `method`, `args`, `result` and `work`. The returned data are separate copies of the saved event and core. Editing a returned row cannot alter the reader's record.

`summary()` returns current counts and work totals, `openingWork()` returns opening checks, and `snapshot()` returns a complete detached record. These three accessors do not consume event slots. The compiler itself returns a record, not a reader:

```js
const { compileAtlas } = require("./equation_atlas.cjs");
const record = compileAtlas({
  source_id: "a-distinct-operation",
  vertices: 6,
  colors: 3
});
```

The delivered constructor used the operation-specific source ID retained in the evidence. Reuse its saved atlas for the same parameter pair; a fresh source ID does not make a repeated compilation new mathematical work.

### Pages and selectors

Pages have `offset`, `limit`, `total`, `next_offset` and `rows`. Offsets default to zero and are positions in the selected sequence; limits default to 64 and must lie in `[0,64]`. An offset equal to the total is permitted and returns an empty page. `next_offset` is null at the end.

A zero-length page before the end returns the same next offset, so an iterator must use a positive limit to advance. For a filtered equation page, offsets index that filtered list rather than global equation IDs. Variable-incidence offsets index one variable's list; term-page offsets index one equation's pairing list.

### Monomial lookup and incidence

`locateMonomial` accepts variable IDs in any order, with at most 12 entries. It returns `member:false` for a wrong degree, overlapping vertices or incomplete coverage. A repeated variable necessarily overlaps vertices. An out-of-range ID is an argument error. For a valid complete matching, it reads the saved pairing index and the saved term at the derived coloring/pairing location.

`jointIncidence` accepts at most 12 distinct variable IDs. It intersects saved ordered incidence lists, using one as candidates and binary membership checks in the others. The empty list selects every term. Shared endpoints can make an intersection empty even though each variable has a nonempty individual incidence list.

The full incidence arrays remain in the core independently of which pages are requested. The actual reader requested the first 64 and final 51 entries of variable `0`'s 243-entry list; the middle 128 were not exported again by that query sequence.

### Formal derivatives and exact expansions

`differentiate` treats its variable list as a sequence of formal partial derivatives. It accepts at most 12 entries. Because all amplitude monomials are square-free, repeating an ID gives the zero polynomial. More derivatives than the monomial degree also give zero. Otherwise it retains terms containing every selected variable and removes those variables from each term.

An empty derivative list returns the original polynomial with its original `rhs`. A nonempty list has constant derivative `rhs:0`. The field `degree_bound` is an upper bound, including for the zero polynomial; it does not assign a nonstandard degree to zero. A remaining empty variable list represents the constant monomial one.

**Formal differentiation does not turn a pointwise constraint `P(x)=b` into an additional constraint that the derivative must vanish.** Derivative records describe polynomials. They are useful for coefficients, Jacobians and algebraic inspection, and are not extra equations imposed on a candidate solution. For example, the actual third derivative below equals one; this does not contradict the original mixed-color equation having right-hand side zero.

For a single variable, the square-free form permits the exact decomposition

$$
P=P_{\text{without }x}+x\,\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}.
$$

`linearExpansion` provides a complete exact rewrite organized by a vertex. Every matching contains exactly one edge at that vertex, so it partitions all terms into groups

$$
P_\alpha=\sum_{x\text{ incident to }v}x\,Q_{\alpha,v,x}.
$$

Each group's `coefficient_terms` is the saved cofactor polynomial, and the original right-hand side is retained. Unlike imposing vanishing derivatives, this grouped expression is an equivalent rewrite of the original equation. Full derivative/expansion output is capped at 256 terms.

### Channel restrictions

`restrictionProfile` accepts a distinct list of channels to set to zero, with at most the model's variable count. It scans the saved monomials, removes every term containing a selected channel, and returns the surviving-term count for every equation. It also returns a histogram, totals split by equation kind, the number of mixed singleton equations, and `impossible_unit_equations`.

An impossible unit equation has lost every term while retaining right-hand side one. That is a direct obstruction to the specified channel restriction. No such obstruction occurred in the delivered restriction profile.

A surviving formal monomial has not been evaluated. Several surviving terms may cancel after complex weights are assigned. A mixed equation with one surviving term requires that product to vanish, but the profile does not solve all resulting conditions jointly.

### Symmetry direction and endpoint order

`vertex_images[v]` is the new label of old vertex `v`. `color_images[c]` is the new label of color `c`, applied globally at every vertex. Both arrays must be permutations of their full ranges. The transformed word satisfies

$$
\alpha'_{\pi(v)}=\sigma(\alpha_v).
$$

For a variable on `u<v`, the reader maps endpoints and colors together. If `pi(u)>pi(v)`, it swaps the endpoint-color coordinates when restoring increasing endpoint order. Each mapped term is located in the saved atlas; the result includes all target term IDs.

These vertex permutations and a single global color permutation preserve the constant-color target. Independent color permutations at individual vertices are not accepted by this API: they generally change which color words are constant.

### Polynomial text

`formatEquation` uses `form:"equation"` by default, producing a sum followed by `= rhs`. `form:"residual"` produces `P-rhs` as an expression, including `- 1` for a constant-color equation. It does not add an equality to that residual expression.

The variable prefix defaults to `x` and must be an identifier of 1–32 ASCII letters/digits/underscores, beginning with a letter or underscore. Products use `*` and terms use `+`. The result retains the original right-hand side and residual constant as separate metadata.

### Monochromatic-edge restriction

Event 16 removes exactly those variables whose two endpoint colors differ. This leaves 45 channels with matching endpoint colors, still as formal variables. Its complete support histogram is:

| Terms left in an equation | Equations | Constant-color equations | Mixed equations |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 546 | 0 | 546 |

| 3 | 90 | 0 | 90 |

There are **405 surviving terms**: 45 in constant-color equations and 360 in mixed equations. All 729 individual support counts are saved, and `impossible_unit_equations` is empty.

The shape has a direct explanation. With only matching endpoint colors, each color must occur an even number of times in a supported coloring. The three single-color words each retain all 15 pairings. A color multiplicity pattern `4+2` has 90 labeled colorings, each with three pairings within the four-vertex color class. Pattern `2+2+2` has 90 colorings, each with one pairing. Every other coloring has an odd color multiplicity and loses all terms.

This is a restriction of formal support. It leaves 90 mixed equations with one product and 90 with three products, together with the three unit-amplitude equations. It does not decide whether their complex weights can simultaneously meet the targets. In particular, the absence of an immediate `0=1` equation is not an existence result.
