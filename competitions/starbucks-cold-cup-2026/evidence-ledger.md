# Evidence ledger

This ledger separates *source facts* from *ClearFiber Shell inferences*. A source can support a mechanism without proving the challenge requirement.

## E1 — Challenge authority

**Source:** Starbucks Global Cold Cup Innovation Challenge, Innocentive  
https://www.innocentive.com/challenges/starbucks-global-cold-cup-innovation-challenge/

**Use:** authoritative challenge requirements, deadline, award/IP framing, TRL range, form fields, maximum three submissions, and statement that solely-generative-AI submissions are not of interest.

**Bound facts used here:** <=5% haze per ASTM D1003; plastic-free definition excludes fossil polymers and plant-based bioplastics that mimic plastics; BPI path; food-contact path; 24 h liquid integrity; 1 m filled drop; LCA categories; scale/forming; prohibited-substance list; deadline Sep 14 2026 23:59 US Eastern; written proposal.

## E2 — CNF/chitosan wet strength + optical precedent

**Source:** Toivonen MS et al. Water-Resistant, Transparent Hybrid Nanopaper by Physical Cross-Linking with Chitosan. *Biomacromolecules* 2015;16:1062–1071.  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.biomac.5b00145  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25665073/

**Bound facts used:** physical cross-linking of CNF/chitosan; reported ~70–90% visible transmittance; 80/20 CNF/chitosan wet ultimate strength ~100 MPa after soaking; dry strength ~200 MPa at 50% RH.

**Not established:** ASTM D1003 haze <=5%; food-contact suitability; 24 h formed-cup leak; drop; heat; BPI.

## E3 — NFC/chitosan wet reinforcement

**Source:** Wu T et al. Mechanical behavior of transparent nanofibrillar cellulose-chitosan nanocomposite films in dry and wet conditions. *J Mech Behav Biomed Mater.* 2014;32:279–286.  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmbbm.2014.01.014  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24508714/

**Bound facts used:** transparent NFC/chitosan films; NFC substantially improved wet mechanical properties.

**Not established:** Starbucks cup requirements.

## E4 — Shellac moisture barrier on cellulose

**Source:** Poddar D et al. Structure-property relationships in shellac-coated paper: impact of coating parameters on high-barrier bio-based packaging applications. *Int J Biol Macromol.* 2025;310(Pt 2):143172.  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2025.143172

**Bound facts used:** shellac coating reduced porosity/roughness; strongest multilayer condition reported WVTR ~56 g m^-2 day^-1 and Cobb ~1 g m^-2.

**Not established:** clear formed cup, <=5% haze, 24 h liquid, drop, final food contact.

## E5 — Shellac coating water-uptake precedent

**Source:** Poddar D et al. Streamlining electrospun shellac coatings on paper for high moisture strength and barrier food packaging film. *Int J Biol Macromol.* 2025;297:139855.  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2025.139855  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39826740/

**Bound facts used:** optimized shellac coating on paper; reported >90% reduction in water absorption and improved moisture barrier.

## E6 — FDA purified shellac inventory

**Source:** FDA Food Contact Substance inventory — purified shellac, CAS 9000-59-3.  
https://hfpappexternal.fda.gov/scripts/fdcc/index.cfm?id=SHELLACPURIFIED&set=IndirectAdditives

**Bound facts used:** purified shellac appears in FDA inventory with 21 CFR citations including 175.105, 175.300, 175.380 and 175.390.

**Important boundary:** this is not a claim that the final ClearFiber Shell article is authorized for Starbucks' intended conditions. Exact use conditions and migration/regulatory route need expert review.

## E7 — BPI certification route

**Source:** BPI Compostability Certification  
https://bpiworld.org/compostability-certification

**Bound facts used:** BPI uses ASTM D6400 as a base standard; ASTM D8410 assessment is available for eligible cellulosic-fiber-based packaging and excludes thermoplastic-laminated fiber articles.

**Source:** BPI New Certification  
https://bpiworld.org/new-certification

**Bound facts used:** certification requires formulation/supplier documentation and can take many months; testing is a substantive development activity, not a paperwork assertion.

## E8 — Optional citric-acid cross-linking precedent

**Source:** Highly Transparent Cross-Linked Cellulose Nanocrystal Coatings with High Gas Barrier and Enhanced Water Resistance for Sustainable Food Packaging. *ACS Applied Polymer Materials* 2026.  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1021/acsapm.6c00129

**Use:** only a contingency precedent for natural-acid cross-linking. It is not the baseline formulation and is not evidence for final haze, cup forming or BPI performance.

## Excluded formulation routes

- **Chlorine-bleach / hypochlorite oxidation** is deliberately excluded even when literature reports strong water-resistant cellulose/chitosan films, because the challenge prohibits measurable chlorine bleach.
- **Polyurethane, PLA/PHA and other synthetic or conventional bioplastic barrier layers** are excluded from the core concept because Starbucks' challenge definition is stricter than ordinary “bio-based plastic.”
- “Transparent” in a paper is **not** treated as equivalent to <=5% haze.
