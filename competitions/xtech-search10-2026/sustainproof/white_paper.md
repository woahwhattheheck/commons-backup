## [INTRODUCTION-5] Problem and proposed capability

Distributed maintenance and supply teams routinely work across disconnected or intermittently connected environments. The hard failure is not simply “lack of data.” It is uncertainty about which local observation is current, whether two teams are acting on the same work order, whether a required part is really available, and whether a later synchronization event superseded or forked an earlier state. Ordinary dashboards can make stale information look authoritative; ordinary messaging can duplicate a maintenance action after reconnection.

**SustainProof** is a local-first evidence reconciliation layer for maintenance and supply handoffs. Each site can record work-order, parts, and inspection state while disconnected. Every state transition carries a monotone generation, a previous-event digest, an observation timestamp, and an evidence digest. On synchronization, SustainProof reconstructs each chain, rejects gaps and same-generation forks, refuses future or stale evidence, and emits a deterministic human-review handoff packet. It never dispatches a maintainer, orders a part, changes an inventory system, or closes a work order itself.

## [TECHNICAL-40] Technical approach

The current prototype is dependency-free Python and operates entirely offline. Its reconciliation kernel performs five concrete controls. First, exact duplicate events collapse while changed content under the same event ID fails closed. Second, each `(stream, subject)` history must begin at generation 1 and advance without gaps. Third, every successor must bind the exact digest of its predecessor, so a later event cannot silently adopt a different history. Fourth, two semantically different events at the same generation create a visible fork instead of lexical last-write-wins. Fifth, freshness is evaluated against process time for current review or an explicitly historical integrity time for deterministic replay.

Work-order review composes maintenance state with parts and inspection evidence. A work order waiting on a part stays on hold unless an independently reconciled parts chain reports at least one available unit. An inspection marked REINSPECT or HOLD prevents a ready handoff. Every output binds the input digest, selected source generation, selected event digest, evaluation mode, reasons, and a receipt digest. All external authority bits remain false. This is intentionally a decision-evidence plane, not an autonomous maintenance controller.

The design fits intermittent operations because reconciliation is deterministic, small, auditable, and does not depend on continuous cloud access. The same packet can be regenerated from retained evidence after reconnection, while conflicting histories remain explicit rather than being overwritten by arrival order.

## [TECHNICAL-40] Phase-I development plan and measurable feasibility

Phase I would turn the prototype into a measured sustainment interoperability layer around real Army-approved maintenance and supply schemas supplied through an authorized customer. The work would preserve the local-first kernel while adding adapters around data rather than embedding one vendor’s semantics in the trust core. Proposed work packages are: (1) define a sponsor-approved event profile for maintenance, parts, and inspection records; (2) build import/export adapters with exact provenance and no silent field loss; (3) implement bounded offline queueing and resynchronization under packet loss, delay, reordering, duplicate delivery, and clock skew; (4) measure reconciliation correctness and operator review burden; and (5) demonstrate recovery from split-history and stale-state scenarios on representative, non-sensitive datasets.

Feasibility metrics are designed to be falsifiable. Target measures for an Army/customer-approved test corpus would include: 100% detection of injected same-generation semantic forks; 100% rejection of intentionally broken predecessor chains; zero duplicate work-order handoffs from exact replay; byte-identical reports under event-order permutations; explicit stale/future evidence holds; and end-to-end reconciliation latency and memory measured at selected dataset sizes. These are proposed test objectives, not measured Army results. The checked-in carrier already contains deterministic synthetic hostiles for the core invariants and is engineering evidence only.

## [ARMY-BENEFITS-25] Army benefit

The operational value is higher confidence in “what changed, who observed it, and what still requires human action” when teams cannot assume a continuously consistent enterprise backend. SustainProof is intended to reduce three costly classes of ambiguity: duplicated maintenance effort after reconnection, maintenance scheduled against stale parts information, and handoffs that lose the causal chain connecting an observation to the state presented to the next team.

The approach is deliberately compatible with human authority. A mechanic, supply specialist, or supervisor receives a compact evidence packet identifying selected generations and explicit HOLD reasons. No autonomous dispatch, purchase, maintenance action, or provider write is authorized by the kernel. That boundary makes the prototype suitable for incremental evaluation beside existing systems: it can begin as an evidence/reconciliation layer rather than demanding a rip-and-replace deployment.

The same architecture can support resilience objectives beyond one maintenance system because subject chains and evidence digests are schema-bounded but transport-neutral. The proposed Army work would test where that portability helps and where domain-specific adapters are necessary. Claims about integration with any named Army system remain **OWNER EVIDENCE REQUIRED** until an authorized customer supplies the interface and evaluation context.

## [COMMERCIAL-25] Commercial potential

The commercial thesis is a reconciliation product for field-service, industrial maintenance, utilities, logistics, and multi-site operations where work crosses weak-connectivity boundaries. The buyer problem is familiar: a team may have multiple systems of record, offline technicians, third-party inventory providers, and asynchronous handoffs, yet still needs a defensible account of which state was used for the next decision. SustainProof can be packaged as an embedded SDK plus operator-facing evidence service, with adapters sold around existing CMMS/ERP and field-service systems instead of replacing them.

A credible commercialization plan needs evidence rather than invented traction. The first civilian wedge should therefore be a narrow paid diagnostic/pilot: ingest one closed historical period of maintenance and parts events, replay synchronization hazards offline, quantify duplicate/stale/fork findings, and deliver a reconciliation report plus adapter plan. Customer interviews, willingness-to-pay, deployment cost, security requirements, and integration time must be measured. Named customers, signed pilots, recognized revenue, savings, and market-share claims are **OWNER EVIDENCE REQUIRED** and are intentionally absent from this draft.

A Phase I Army effort would strengthen commercial value by forcing the product to operate under harsher disconnected and provenance requirements than typical SaaS workflows. Reusable outputs would be the bounded reconciliation kernel, adapter contract, test harness for duplicate/reorder/fork scenarios, operator evidence format, and performance measurements. Army-specific interfaces or restricted data would remain customer-controlled and would not become public product claims.

## [PROPOSAL-QUALITY-5] Execution, risk, and current readiness

Primary technical risks are incomplete source histories, clock skew, schema drift, and operator overload from excessive HOLD states. The mitigation is explicit measurement: retain source generations, separate historical replay from current review, make every hold reason machine-readable, and tune adapters only with sponsor-approved evidence. A second risk is false authority: a correct reconciliation receipt could be mistaken for authorization to dispatch, procure, or submit. The prototype hard-codes all such authority false and the proposal keeps human/system-of-record authority outside the kernel.
